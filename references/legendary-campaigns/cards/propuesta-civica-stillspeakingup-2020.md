# #StillSpeakingUp (Propuesta Cívica & RSF · 2020)

## Insight
Investigating corruption and the drug trade has cost 130+ Mexican journalists their lives since 2007, and every murder produces more silence — in some states there are no voices left to speak freely, handing criminal organizations and corrupt officials the upper hand. Mexicans, living among the dead, have lost sensitivity to murder news. Which leads to the campaign's brutal central truth: the only journalist who can speak up without fear in Mexico is a dead one.

## Mechanic
Using AI, the campaign brought back Javier Valdez — the iconic investigative journalist murdered in 2017 — recreating his image exactly and his voice from recordings of male members of his own family. Instead of falling silent, Javier became a face and voice behind whom living colleagues could anonymously continue speaking up. The reveal was engineered for maximum resonance: Mexico's most respected journalists were gathered on a Zoom call with no agenda and shown the video cold; their raw emotional reactions turned them into ambassadors. Then, on Día de Muertos — the day the dead return to visit their families — Javier addressed Mexico's president directly on national TV, simultaneously across all channels, under the line: "They try to silence us, but we're #StillSpeakingUp."

## Why it worked
P18 (Tech as Canvas): AI resurrection wasn't a gimmick — it was the only mechanism that could deliver a message no living person could safely deliver. P05 (Cultural Hijack): anchoring the reveal to Día de Muertos converted a deepfake from uncanny to sacred; within Mexican tradition, a dead man returning to demand justice is culturally coherent. P13 (Truth Telling): the campaign named the president as its addressee and the state's complacency as the problem. Results were structural, not just media: all three main news channels gave the video prime time, 100+ international outlets published it, 349M organic impressions with 84% positive sentiment — and under the media pressure, stalled trials were reactivated, leading to six murderers convicted for crimes against journalists in Mexico for the first time. It became a platform: using murdered journalists' identities to protect those at risk today.

## Steal
When fear silences every living messenger, find the messenger who has nothing left to lose — the dead, the anonymous, the already-exiled — and build the tech to give them a voice. Then anchor the reveal to the cultural ritual that makes it legitimate rather than ghoulish. And seed the story with the audience's own gatekeepers first (the journalists), so the media isn't covering your campaign — it is your campaign.
