# It Has to Be Heinz (Heinz · 2019)

## Insight
After 150 years, Heinz risked losing its iconic status to off-brand competitors — but claiming superiority from the top down would only sound like every other legacy brand defending its turf. The stronger truth was already out in the world: consumers behave as if Heinz *is* ketchup. Ask anyone to draw ketchup and they draw the keystone label. The brand didn't need to say it; it needed to engineer moments where the world said it for them.

## Mechanic
A five-year platform of serialized proof-points, each harvested from real consumer behavior. Pour Perfectly tilted the label to reveal the perfect 31° pour angle. During COVID lockdowns, a 570-piece all-red Heinz puzzle. Draw Ketchup asked people to draw ketchup — everyone drew Heinz. When AI image tools trended, they asked DALL-E to draw ketchup — even AI drew Heinz. Hot Dog Pact rallied a national petition to fix uneven hot-dog and bun packs (Wonder Bread capitulated). Ketchup Fraud exposed restaurants refilling Heinz bottles with inferior ketchup. When a fan account posted Taylor Swift eating chicken with "ketchup and seemingly ranch," Heinz relaunched Kranch as "Heinz Ketchup and Seemingly Ranch" within days. Super Bowl LVII was hacked with one fact: LVII = 57, the Heinz number. Results: +12% global sales growth since 2019, +3.2 share points taken from competitors, dollar share 72.6% → 74.1%, retail sales $578MM → $851MM, KANTAR 'Icon' status in 2023.

## Why it worked
P13 (Truth Telling): every execution externalizes one verifiable truth — the world already treats Heinz as the category itself — rather than asserting a claim. P09 (User as Co-Author): the audience literally generates the proof (drawings, petition signatures, fan posts); the brand just frames it. P10 (Serialization & Ritual): five years of repeated, formatted proof compounds into iconic status the way a single campaign never could — each new execution deposits into the same platform account. The genius is the reversal of the burden of proof: bottom-up evidence beats top-down superiority claims, and cultural moments (AI, Swift, Super Bowl) become free distribution for the same single-minded thought.

## Steal
When your brand is the default of its category, stop advertising the claim and start engineering demonstrations where consumers prove it themselves. Build a platform elastic enough that any cultural moment can be converted into the same proof — then keep depositing for years, not quarters.
