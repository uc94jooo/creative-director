# Room for Everyone (Mastercard · 2024)

## Insight
After millions of Ukrainians fled to Poland, initial solidarity faded into economic anxiety and social tension — "we have a second nation to support." With ~1.5 million Ukrainians still in Poland, the perception problem had a factual answer: Ukrainian-owned businesses add value to the Polish economy. And a deeper commercial insight sat underneath: entrepreneurs have always feared competition, but complementary businesses next to each other — a restaurant beside a beauty salon — drive each other's growth. Data could prove there is room for everyone.

## Mechanic
Extending its Where to Settle platform, Mastercard partnered with Gratka (Poland's leading real estate portal) to build a tool that uses aggregated, anonymized transaction data, foot traffic, air quality and business-complementarity insights to show Ukrainian entrepreneurs the optimal locations to open next to Polish businesses — to mutual benefit. Owners input their business type and location preferences; the platform pinpoints spots where a new business would complement existing ones (a bookstore near a jewelry store). The design was grounded in qualitative research with micro-entrepreneurs on how they actually choose locations. Promotion featured Polish business owners personally inviting Ukrainian entrepreneurs to open next door — storefront posters, digital, social, PR and OOH.

## Why it worked
P04 (Brand as Activist): Mastercard took a position on a divisive social tension, but backed it with infrastructure rather than sentiment — "doing well by doing good" executed as a working tool. P03 (Behavior Inversion): the campaign flips competition, the thing entrepreneurs fear, into the thing they should actively seek — and flips the refugee narrative from burden to business partner. P14 (Product as Proof): Mastercard's actual asset — transaction data — is the campaign; the argument for coexistence is made by the tool working. Results: 12,000 users in the first month, up to 40% of new business owners using the platform, a 10-point lift in Poles agreeing Ukrainian businesses benefit the economy, and a 21-point lift in perceiving Mastercard as a brand that supports entrepreneurs.

## Steal
When public sentiment turns against a group, don't run an empathy ad — build a tool that makes cooperation with that group visibly profitable. Data the brand already owns, repackaged as a decision-making utility, argues more persuasively than any manifesto film.
