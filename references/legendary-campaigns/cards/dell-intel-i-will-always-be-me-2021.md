# I Will Always Be Me (Dell Technologies & Intel · 2021)

## Insight
Almost everyone living with motor neurone disease will lose their voice, and voice banking lets them keep a digital copy of it — yet only about 12% of people with MND in the UK had banked theirs. The old process demanded recording ~1,600 clinical phrases over an average of three months; people gave up, and denial kept the newly diagnosed from confronting a future without their voice at the exact moment banking works best. The voice is not audio data — it is identity, and the last channel of self-expression MND leaves intact.

## Mechanic
With the MND Association and Rolls-Royce, Dell and Intel built the world's first voice-banking book. New York Times bestselling author Jill Twiss wrote, and Nicholas Stevenson illustrated, a story that a person with MND reads aloud to their loved ones — a gentle way to tell their family what is happening ("everything is changing, but I will always be me"). The text is engineered to contain every syllable combination needed to synthesize an accurate digital voice, and the emotion of reading to family produces a higher-quality voice model than sterile phrase lists. The story lives at iwillalwaysbeme.com, the first AAA-accessible voice-banking platform, working from any laptop or phone. Voice-banking rates rose 50% shortly after launch; it became one of the UK's leading banking methods, expanding to Ireland, Australia and the US. Pharma Grand Prix, Cannes 2022.

## Why it worked
P01 (Format as Idea): the entire idea is the format swap — a clinical data-capture chore reframed as a children's-book bedtime ritual; the medium does the persuading. P14 (Product as Proof): the tech partnership's "technology drives humanity forward" belief is demonstrated, not claimed — the product is the campaign. P06 (Limitation as Power): the constraint (all phonemes must appear in a readable story) becomes the craft achievement, and the emotional context of reading aloud isn't a side effect, it improves the technical output. Two dreaded tasks — banking your voice and telling your family — are dissolved into one act of love.

## Steal
When adoption of a helpful behavior is low, don't advertise the behavior — redesign the experience that makes people avoid it, and hide the hard task inside an act people already want to perform. Ask: what data do we need, and what emotionally meaningful ritual could capture it as a by-product?
