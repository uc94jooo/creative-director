# The Final Copy of Ilon Specht (L'Oréal Paris · 2024)

## Insight
In 1971, advertising was written from a male point of view — on the L'Oréal assignment, the agency insisted on a male voiceover describing a woman who "uses the most expensive hair color." Copywriter Ilon Specht knew it was wrong, got angry, and refused to write anything about looking good for men. Out of that anger came four words — "Because I'm Worth It" — a feminist manifesto disguised as a tagline, now the longest-running ad copy in history, translated into 40 languages. Yet almost nobody knew its origin story, or the woman behind it. With misogyny resurgent in 2025's culture, telling that story became the point.

## Mechanic
L'Oréal Paris, McCann and IPG Mediabrands' Traverse32 commissioned two-time Academy Award winner Ben Proudfoot to direct an intimate short documentary chronicling Specht's life and legacy — including final interviews with Specht herself, who insists on camera: "I'm not interested in advertising... It's about humans. It's about caring for people because we're all worth it or no one is worth it." The film works as standalone cinema — winning the Director Award at BrandStorytelling 2025 — while reclaiming a defining asset of the brand's history: the slogan wasn't created to sell anything; it was her truth, and she lived it.

## Why it worked
P13 (Truth Telling): the film derives its power from documentary honesty — the sexism of the original brief, Specht's fury, her refusal — which retroactively recharges a 50-year-old tagline with its original defiance. P16 (Long-form Drama): only a real film, made by an Oscar-winning director with cinematic integrity, could earn festival credibility and cultural conversation no ad could buy. P08 (Craft as Message): honoring advertising craft — a copywriter as protagonist — lets the brand celebrate its own asset without self-congratulation, because the hero is the author, not the client. The timing (against a "masculine energy" cultural backlash) made a heritage story feel urgent rather than nostalgic.

## Steal
Audit your brand's oldest assets for buried origin stories: who made them, against what resistance, and what did that fight mean? A tagline audiences have heard ten thousand times can be re-heard once you reveal the human defiance that produced it — and a documentary about the author beats an anniversary ad about the brand.
