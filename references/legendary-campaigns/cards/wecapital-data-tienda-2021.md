# Data Tienda (WeCapital · 2021)

## Insight
83% of low-income Mexican women have no bank credit history, so their loan applications are rejected — they cannot study or start businesses. The paradox: these same women have received informal credit from neighborhood stores (tienditas) all their lives, borrowing for their family's livelihood and paying back on time week after week. They already have long, disciplined credit histories — the records just live in shopkeepers' handwritten accounting notebooks instead of the financial information bureaus. More than 35 million women were invisible to banks while being creditworthy in plain sight.

## Mechanic
WeCapital built datatienda.mx, a financial information center within reach of banks that recovers women's credit histories from neighborhood-store records. A woman enters at least five trusted stores where she has bought on credit; the system then uses WhatsApp bots to automatically collect qualitative and quantitative payment data from those shopkeepers and generates a credit score that analyses even more variables than the traditional system. The comms budget matched the audience: loudspeaker campaigns in working-class neighborhoods of Mexico City, then Puebla, Guadalajara, Morelia, and Monterrey. Within months: 10,300+ credit histories recovered from the records of ~50,000 shopkeepers nationwide, and 23% of registered women (2,300+) received their first microloans (MX$2,000–20,000) for businesses and study plans.

## Why it worked
P06 (Limitation as Power): the barrier ("no credit history") was reframed as a data-location problem — the history existed all along in an overlooked medium, and the idea simply built the bridge between informal and formal finance. P14 (Product as Proof): WeCapital's mission of financial inclusion is executed as working infrastructure, not asserted in ads; every recovered history is the campaign. The craft choices respect the audience's reality end to end — WhatsApp bots because shopkeepers already use WhatsApp, loudspeakers because that's the neighborhood's actual media channel. Banks win too: the score reduces their risk margin, making inclusion commercially sustainable rather than charitable.

## Steal
When a system excludes people for "lacking data," assume the data exists in an informal medium the system doesn't read — then build the translator, not an awareness campaign. And deliver through the channels your audience genuinely uses (a loudspeaker beats a hashtag when that's where trust lives).
