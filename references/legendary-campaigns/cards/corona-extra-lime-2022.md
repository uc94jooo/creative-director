# Corona Extra Lime (Corona · 2022)

## Insight
In China — the world's largest beer-drinking market — Corona's growth was driven by its lime ritual, supplied by imported high-quality Tahitian limes. Covid broke the supply chain, threatening the ritual the brand is built on. At the same time, 24% of local farmers lived in poverty. Two problems, one solution: instead of fixing the shortage by importing more limes, grow your own — and let the farmers own the upside.

## Mechanic
Corona partnered with local governments, industry authorities, and lime farmers, transferring advanced cultivation knowledge to raise Chinese limes to the highest quality standard. After 1,000 days of research, development, and farming, a completely new product brand — Corona Extra Lime — hit shelves across China, sold in bundle packs with all profits redirected to the farmers to expand their farms and improve living conditions. Corona shifted half its media from online to offline to promote the limes, turning the fruit itself into an unprecedented brand medium. Committed scale-up for 2024: +209% lime yield, +130% cultivated area, +30% farmer per-capita income.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): the brand's most famous ritual was defended not with advertising but with an actual second product — the lime became simultaneously supply chain, CSR program, and media channel. P04 (Brand as Activist): the social commitment was structural (profits to farmers, multi-year agronomy investment), making the purpose claim self-evidencing. It converted a supply threat into the brand's biggest single-market commitment ever. Results: $11M earned media, 1B+ impressions, +21% farmer per-capita income, +17% national brand power, +29% beer sales (2022 vs 2020), 2M+ limes sold yearly, 100% positive or neutral sentiment. Cannes Titanium Lion 2023.

## Steal
When your brand depends on a ritual, treat the ritual's weakest supply link as brand territory: securing it can be the campaign. Solving your business problem and a community's problem with the same mechanism beats donating to either — and a physical product that carries the story is a media channel competitors can't buy.
