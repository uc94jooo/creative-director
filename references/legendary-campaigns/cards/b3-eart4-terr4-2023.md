# EART4/TERR4 (B3 Stock Exchange & UN Global Compact · 2023)

## Insight
The IPCC says Earth is in a "now or never" moment, but the people positioned to commit entire companies to the UN's goals — business leaders — work an average of 62.5 hours a week and are laser-focused on business. Climate messaging framed as cause communication never reaches them. To grab this audience, the planet had to speak their language: financial language.

## Mechanic
The UN Global Compact and B3 (the "Brazilian Nasdaq," the world's first stock exchange to sign the Compact) turned Earth into a publicly listed company — EART4 — and launched "the most urgent IPO ever," with awful results. The fake company had everything a listed company has: an institutional website with a real-time stock simulator reacting to news about the planet, and an annual report full of hair-raising numbers, mailed to business leaders — all scientific data translated into corporate terms by researchers, journalists and writers. The IPO launched at B3 as a PR stunt, with billboards in business hubs including the Nasdaq Tower in Times Square. "Investing in EART4" meant committing your company to the UN's long-term goals.

## Why it worked
P01 (Format as Idea): the IPO, the ticker, the annual report — the entire grammar of finance becomes the creative vehicle, so the message needs no translation for its audience. P05 (Cultural Hijack): rather than pulling executives out of the business world, the campaign invaded it — business events, business media, business mail — meeting a time-starved target on their own turf. Framing planetary collapse as bankruptcy made urgency legible to people who price risk for a living. Results: 20,000+ leaders reached by the report, 140% growth in new signatories vs 2022, 70% of Brazil's top 100 companies signed the Global Compact, and the IPO is being rolled out on stock exchanges globally. Creative B2B Grand Prix, Cannes Lions 2023.

## Steal
For any hard-to-reach professional audience, translate your cause into the native document formats of their trade (an IPO, a term sheet, an audit, a job spec) — the format does the targeting for you. B2B insight can be as simple as a diary: know exactly how many hours your target works and where their eyes already are.
