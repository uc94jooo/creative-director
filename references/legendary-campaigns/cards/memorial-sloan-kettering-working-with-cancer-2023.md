# Working With Cancer (Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center · 2023)

## Insight
50% of people diagnosed with cancer are afraid to tell their employer — fearing for their job, their healthcare, their career path. So in addition to being scared for their health, they hide their illness at work, going through workdays while secretly struggling. The trigger was personal: when Publicis Groupe CEO Arthur Sadoun shared his own cancer diagnosis publicly, the outpouring of stories revealed how many people never could. Workplace policy changes top-down — so the audience wasn't patients, it was CEOs.

## Mechanic
#WorkingWithCancer called on companies to make a public pledge to end the stigma of cancer at work and back employees with concrete job security. Launch was engineered around leadership moments: phase one at Davos in January 2023 with C-suite-targeted newspaper placements and business-site takeovers, plus leading cancer institutions and founding pledge companies in the US, UK, and France; phase two timed to World Cancer Day and the Super Bowl for mass awareness. The "Work/Life" film dramatized the isolation of working while secretly ill; OOH split faces in two with the 50% statistic. $100M in donated media, Times Square placements, and celebrity support carried it. 390 companies signed — including Fortune 100 firms — covering more than 13 million employees, with 295M+ earned impressions.

## Why it worked
P04 (Brand as Activist): a cancer hospital and a holding company spent their institutional relationships — not just media — to change employer behavior, using Davos as a media channel because that's where the actual decision-makers were. P13 (Truth Telling): one brutal, personally-vouched statistic (50% hide their diagnosis) did the persuasion; the CEO's own diagnosis made corporate leaders unable to dismiss it as someone else's issue. The pledge mechanic converts sympathy into a public, checkable commitment — reputation becomes the enforcement mechanism.

## Steal
To change workplace behavior, don't campaign at employees — campaign at the 400 people who set policy for millions, in the rooms where they gather. And make the ask a public pledge: a signature that's visible is a commitment that's costly to abandon.
