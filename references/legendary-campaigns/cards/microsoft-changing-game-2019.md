# Changing the Game (Microsoft · 2019)

## Insight
Microsoft was seen as a "productivity-enabler" — few knew its mission to empower everyone, or the projects proving it. Buried in the product line was the Xbox Adaptive Controller, built with the accessibility community for the 33 million disabled gamers in the US who otherwise invent workarounds like playing with their face. It had no intended marketing support, was launching during the sales-driven holidays, and nobody knew whether it would halo to the master brand. The creative flip: don't treat gamers with disabilities as the audience — make them the campaign.

## Mechanic
Real kids with limited mobility — like nine-year-old Owen, born with one hand — starred as themselves, showing how the Adaptive Controller (designed for players with one hand, no hands, or limited fine motor control, with accessible packaging down to the box) puts them on the same playing field as everyone else. Instead of a quiet launch to the disability community, Microsoft took it to the Super Bowl — the year's biggest celebration of physical ability and single-winner competition — as the perfect juxtaposition for the line "when everybody plays, we all win." The spot topped Super Bowl ad rankings and shifted the cultural conversation around inclusive design to a mass audience.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): the values claim rests on shipped hardware co-designed with the community — the film only reports what the product already proves, which is why it reads as truth rather than purpose-washing. P13 (Truth Telling): real children, real names, real fathers voicing real fears ("how will Owen be viewed by the other kids") — unscripted specificity carries the emotion. The media choice is the strategy: placing disability at the center of the Super Bowl reframed inclusion from niche accommodation to mainstream celebration.

## Steal
Search your company's product shelf for the unmarketed item that proves your mission better than any manifesto — then launch it in the most mainstream context available, not the community niche. Contrast between message and media moment (inclusion at the winner-takes-all Super Bowl) multiplies attention.
