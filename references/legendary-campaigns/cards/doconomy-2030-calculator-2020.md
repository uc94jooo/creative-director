# The 2030 Calculator (Doconomy · 2020)

## Insight
Consumers can't shop by carbon footprint if products aren't labeled, and small and medium brands — the ones most eager to compete on sustainability — are locked out of impact transparency because life-cycle assessment is complex, slow, costly and unstandardized. There was no common methodology that let a watch, a brush and a jacket be compared on carbon across brands and categories. Carbon labeling needed to be democratized before impact-aware purchasing could exist at all.

## Mechanic
Doconomy built the 2030 Calculator: a free, simple web tool that lets any manufacturing brand compute the cradle-to-gate carbon footprint of a product with no expert knowledge — enter materials and weights, energy and transport, facility location, and get a comparable footprint score. The demo film shows real small Swedish brands (a watchmaker, a clothing label, a brush maker employing visually impaired craftsmen) calculating their own products live: 16 kg for a garment, 253 g for a bath brush. Built as an open platform with a standardized scoring methodology, it spans apparel, shoes, furniture, household goods and more, and turns low-impact production into a competitive edge for small brands against giants. Hundreds of companies — from start-ups to Global 500 enterprises — adopted it, with 3,000+ calculations performed and recognition from the University of Oxford Net Zero Initiative. Grand Prix at Cannes.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): Doconomy's mission to "rewire the financial system" toward conscious consumption is executed as working infrastructure, not stated as a message — the tool is the campaign. P17 (Design as Idea): the creative leap is radical simplification — collapsing costly LCA consultancy into a form anyone can fill in — and the name hard-wires the UN 2030 deadline into the product itself. Strategically it weaponizes transparency as an underdog advantage: giants can afford opacity, small brands win by disclosure, which gives thousands of companies a self-interested reason to spread the standard.

## Steal
When a virtuous behavior is blocked by cost and complexity, the biggest creative act is building the cheap, standardized version — democratize the capability and let adoption be the campaign. And look for the constituency whose self-interest aligns with your purpose (here: SMEs who win by transparency); they will distribute your idea for free.
