# I Am (Starbucks · 2020)

## Insight
Having your name written on a Starbucks cup, no questions asked, is a small daily act of acceptance — one that transgender people in Brazil cannot count on anywhere else. Brazil has the largest transgender population in the world and also leads the world in violence against them. For someone who has transitioned, legally changing your name is a crucial step for living — jobs, doctors, college all run on documents — yet the notary process is bureaucratic, expensive, humiliating and unwelcoming. The brand ritual (your name, accepted) contained the exact promise the state was failing to keep.

## Mechanic
On Transgender Day of Visibility, Starbucks Brazil transformed an entire São Paulo store into a fully functioning register office, in partnership with the Cerqueira César notary. NGOs, transgender influencers and Starbucks' own trans employees spread the word; on the day, hosts, clerks and baristas greeted participants, served coffee, gave legal advice, heard their stories and conducted the complete legal name-change process free of charge. Each new document was delivered in a box containing a cup with the person's new name written on it and a Starbucks card loaded with R$500. Demand ran 7x the annual São Paulo average for name changes — in a single day — and the response was so strong it became an annual program: every January 29th, Starbucks stores now serve those who want to change their legal names.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): the idea scales the brand's smallest existing behavior — writing your name on a cup — into a legal institution; "your name will not only be written on our cups but in your documents" is the ritual made consequential. P04 (Brand as Activist): rather than a rainbow-washed film, the brand absorbed the bureaucracy, cost and hostility of a real process and replaced it with hospitality, converting purpose from message into service. Becoming an annual program is what separates it from stunt territory — the commitment compounds, and the LGBTQI+ community's bond with the brand strengthened because the help is repeatable, not commemorative.

## Steal
Audit your brand's smallest habitual gesture toward customers and ask what it would mean at institutional scale. The strongest activism ideas take a hostile process the audience must endure, and re-host it inside the brand's most welcoming space — with the brand paying the friction costs.
