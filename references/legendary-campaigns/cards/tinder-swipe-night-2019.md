# Swipe Night (Tinder · 2019)

## Insight
Seven years after inventing the swipe, Tinder faced user fatigue: for Gen Z (over 50% of users, 18–25), swiping had decayed into a mindless pastime, and women especially were disgruntled by the superficiality of profile-based matching. They craved deeper connection without the seriousness of questionnaire dating sites. The team's pivotal realization: the most powerful tool for resetting Gen Z's relationship with Tinder wasn't a marketing campaign — it was Tinder itself. And the route to depth was the adage "actions speak louder than words": what you do under pressure reveals more than what you write in a bio.

## Mechanic
Swipe Night is a first-of-its-kind interactive apocalyptic miniseries inside the Tinder app, live on Sunday nights. Shot in first-person POV for mobile (Tinder's first in-app streaming video), each 5–8 minute episode forces split-second moral choices via the iconic swipe — save the puppy or the person? steal her ride or ask for one? — with seven seconds to go with your gut, across 2,000+ branching storylines built by Gen Z creators, Tinder engineers and a game designer. The choices became compatibility data: post-episode "Night Mode" pooled users who chose alike, and everyone's decisions displayed on their profiles as ready-made icebreakers ("Did you save the puppy? This says a lot about your personality"). Haptic feedback, character push notifications and a live viewer counter made it an appointment event.

## Why it worked
P01 (Format as Idea): the invention is a new format — "Product–Experience–TV Show" — not a message; the hard-to-classify nature was itself the PR. P09 (User as Co-Author): you star in the story, and your authored choices become both matchmaking algorithm and conversation starter, converting entertainment into utility. P18 (Tech as Canvas): in-app streaming plus branching narrative turned the swipe from a judgment on faces into a window into character. Results: viewership beating SNL and The Bachelorette (premiere 4x Euphoria's pilot), +26% matches on a typical Sunday night, +12% messages, 4B earned impressions, +1640% Twitter mentions, international rollout and a second season. Cannes Grand Prix winner (2020/21).

## Steal
When your product's core mechanic has decayed into a habit, don't advertise around the fatigue — build the reinvention inside the product, reusing the mechanic people already know for a new purpose (swipe faces → swipe fates). Design participation so the fun generates data and the data generates the product benefit; the entertainment must pay off in the core use case, not beside it.
