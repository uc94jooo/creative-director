# My Line (Ministry of Communications & Information · 2017)

## Insight
Over 90% of Colombia had mobile coverage, but nearly 20 million people — in remote or impoverished regions — had no internet access or smartphones, digitally excluded from the information everyone else takes for granted. Yet 99.3% of Colombians had access to a phone line, many via old "brick" feature phones. The gap wasn't connectivity; it was the assumption that accessing Google requires a screen and a data plan.

## Mechanic
Instead of building expensive infrastructure, the Ministry and MullenLowe SSP3 turned the Google Assistant into a voice service reachable through a traditional landline number: 6000913. A caller on any legacy phone asks a question aloud; custom software converts voice to text, queries Google's systems in the cloud, and speaks the answer back within a second or two — no data, no Wi-Fi, no smartphone. The line was promoted to encourage adoption, and tens of thousands of calls came in within the first few months, covering local weather, medical advice, and homework help. Search and AI became available to anyone who could dial.

## Why it worked
P06 (Limitation as Power): the legacy phone — the very symbol of digital exclusion — becomes the access device; the solution embraces the constraint instead of waiting for infrastructure to remove it. P18 (Tech as Canvas): a thin custom API stitched existing technologies (telephony, speech-to-text, Google search) into a new public utility, proving that the cheapest creative material is recombination. The idea gave Google effective reach to the 99.3% of Colombians with a phone line but no data plan, and won the Creative Data Grand Prix at Cannes 2018 — simple tech solving a massive social problem.

## Steal
Map what your excluded audience already owns, not what they lack, and port your service onto that installed base. The most scalable inclusion idea is usually a translation layer — old interface in front, modern capability behind — rather than new hardware or new infrastructure.
