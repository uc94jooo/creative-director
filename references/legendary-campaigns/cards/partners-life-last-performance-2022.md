# The Last Performance (Partners Life · 2022)

## Insight
New Zealand is one of the most underinsured countries in the world — only 29% of New Zealanders have life insurance. The reluctance to engage with the category is really a reluctance to engage with death itself, and category research showed advertising was the weakest motivational trigger: competitors sold the "joy and confidence" of being covered, a contrived truth that didn't resonate. Yet the same people who avoid thinking about their own mortality happily consume death as entertainment — true-crime podcasts, murder mysteries, dark comedies. And the core product truth: hindsight is useless when you're dead. Partners Life would give Kiwis the hindsight they need before it's too late — by borrowing it from the dead.

## Mechanic
Partners Life partnered with New Zealand's most popular murder mystery, The Brokenwood Mysteries. At the end of every episode, before the credits, that week's murdered character woke up from the dead — on the morgue slab — for one "last performance": sharing their regrets about not getting life insurance, scripted to each episode's storyline and the character's life. Same actors, same director, same sets, same crew, for an entire six-week season, with scripts approved by the show's creator and head writer. The placement deal ran the scenes *inside* the show as its last scene — making a category everyone avoids completely unavoidable — on free-to-air TV from August to September 2022 and on-demand through year end, under the line "Plan ahead and get life right." Results: 70% of New Zealanders now believe in the importance of life insurance, +9 points in active consideration, brand awareness up to a three-year-high 29%, and website enquiries up 70%.

## Why it worked
P01 (Format as Idea): the media placement is the creativity — an ad that lives inside the show's narrative, shot as a continuation of it, dissolves the ad-avoidance reflex entirely; there was no moment to skip. P05 (Cultural Hijack): rather than dragging audiences to a death conversation, the brand went where the nation was already comfortably engaging with death — its favorite murder mystery — and borrowed the show's trust wholesale. P11 (Absurd as Carrier): corpses sitting up to deliver deadpan regrets ("didn't see this coming") lets ironic humor carry a genuinely morbid message; the laugh opens the door the fear had closed. Honesty did the rest: admitting "we're selling death insurance" out-motivated the category's peace-of-mind clichés.

## Steal
When your category's barrier is a taboo, don't fight the avoidance — find the entertainment format where your audience already engages with the taboo voluntarily, and integrate there with full craft fidelity (same director, same actors, same world). And interrogate what you're really selling: naming the uncomfortable truth your competitors euphemize is itself a differentiating strategy.
