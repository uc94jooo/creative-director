# True Name (Mastercard · 2019)

## Insight
For Trans and Nonbinary people, the name printed on a credit card is not a formality — it's a safety issue. 32% of transgender people who've shown an ID with a name or gender that didn't match their presentation have been harassed, denied service, or assaulted. The insight came from inside the agency: a Trans creative whose dead name was called out loud by a deli merchant, exposing him in public. A fundamental element of every payment card was broken for millions — and Mastercard, which makes cards work but doesn't issue them, couldn't fix it alone.

## Mechanic
True Name is the first payment product letting people put their chosen name on their card — no legal name change required. When Mastercard pitched it, no bank would commit. So Mastercard announced the product anyway, at World Pride NYC 2019, through a 360 campaign of advocate panels, Pride broadcast advertising, PR, social, and OOH — publicly announcing a card it could not make, to force the market to make it. The cultural response secured issuing partners (BMO Harris, Superbia Credit Union, Citibank, Bunq), and competitors including Visa and JP Morgan Chase subsequently changed their own practices — a new industry standard.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): the commitment isn't a rainbow logo — it's a functional change to the physical product at the center of the brand's business. P04 (Brand as Activist): Mastercard spent its industry relationships, not just its media budget, putting its reputation on the line against its own partners. P13 (Truth Telling): the launch film leads with real, frightening testimony about dead-naming, grounding a product feature in physical stakes. Announcing an unmakeable product was the strategic masterstroke — it converted public pressure into supply-chain leverage.

## Steal
When your brand is an intermediary that can't fix the problem alone, announce the fix anyway and let demand force your partners' hands. And when supporting a community, change what your product does for them before you change what your advertising says about them.
