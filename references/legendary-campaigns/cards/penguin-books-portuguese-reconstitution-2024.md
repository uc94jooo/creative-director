# Portuguese (Re)Constitution (Penguin Books · 2024)

## Insight
Penguin Random House is the world's most global publisher, but in Portugal it was not seen as a brand connected to local culture. Meanwhile, Portugal carries a very specific cultural wound: for over four decades of fascist dictatorship, the blue pencil was the censor's tool — the physical instrument that struck words from artists' work. A publisher cannot prove love for a culture with an ad; it can only prove it with a book. And the most powerful book possible would be one that turns the regime's own instrument of censorship into an instrument of creation.

## Mechanic
On the 50th anniversary of the Carnation Revolution, Penguin Books gave blue pencils to Portuguese contemporary artists. Using the blackout poetry technique, poets spent six months finding poems about freedom and love hidden inside the words of the fascist constitution itself. Then 11 illustrators spent another six months covering the remaining words with original blue-pencil illustrations — each receiving a page with the poets' words already selected, which is why wildly different illustration styles share remarkable synchrony. The result: 58 original illustrations, a 2,500-copy first print run launched on the anniversary of the revolution, sold in the country's leading bookstore chains.

## Why it worked
P17 (Design as Idea): the object is the campaign — the concept, the medium, and the message are physically inseparable; the blue pencil that censored becomes the pencil that draws. P08 (Craft as Message): a full year of production, with the constraint (only words present in the fascist constitution, only blue pencil) doing the persuasive work — the craft discipline is the proof of respect for the culture. P05 (Cultural Hijack): it commandeered a national moment (the 50th anniversary) and a national symbol (the blue pencil) rather than inventing something new. The results validated the strategy: bestseller status with the first edition sold out, 7.25M OTS of earned media with zero media spend, entry into the Portuguese National Reading Plan, use in schools to teach revolution history through art, and the Design Grand Prix at Cannes. A brand that wanted to be seen as culturally Portuguese produced an artifact the Portuguese state itself adopted as an educational tool.

## Steal
When a brand needs to prove cultural belonging, don't say it — make a cultural object the culture itself wants to keep. Look for the instrument of the wound (the censor's pencil, the propaganda leaflet, the border fence) and invert its function. If your constraint is meaningful enough, the craft becomes the story and the press writes the media plan for you.
