# Sick Beats (Woojer · 2020)

## Insight
Roughly 100,000 people worldwide — mostly children — live with cystic fibrosis, which fills the lungs with thick mucus. Daily airway-clearance vests are non-negotiable, but the technology hadn't meaningfully improved in 20+ years: bulky, loud, medical-looking, $10,000+ machines that mechanically beat the chest for 20 minutes at a time. Kids described the treatment as "the worst part of my day," which made them less likely to do it. The scientific unlock: 40Hz soundwaves are clinically proven to be as effective as traditional vests — the frequency matches the harmonic resonance of mucus and liquifies it. And 40Hz already exists inside the music kids love.

## Mechanic
Sick Beats by Woojer is the world's first music-powered airway-clearance vest for CF. The vest syncs to a smartphone and extracts the 40Hz frequencies from whatever the wearer is listening to, delivering them to the chest in real time — treatment becomes listening to music ("it feels like I'm inside a speaker"). The team scanned Spotify's catalog of millions of songs to identify tracks containing the 40Hz tone and gave the CF community dedicated access to the therapeutic library via the Sick Beats Spotify profile, with curated playlists across genres. The launch strategy was itself engineered: a pre-launch publicity push through the awards circuit (Cannes Lions Grand Prix wins, Clio Grand Prix, Ad Age Tech Innovation of the Year, Fast Company's #4 most innovative) plus a partnership with Claire's Place Foundation to win over the gated CF community — designed to recruit the physicians needed for clinical trials and the ~$4MM investment needed to launch at scale.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): this is purpose executed as engineering — the brand didn't advertise empathy for CF kids, it built the device that fixes their worst daily moment, with clinically validated efficacy at its core. P18 (Tech as Canvas): Spotify's existing catalog became the medical supply chain; scanning millions of songs for a therapeutic frequency turned a streaming platform into a treatment library at zero marginal cost. P06 (Limitation as Power): the constraint — treatment must happen daily, for 20 minutes, on the chest — was reframed as a listening session, converting compliance from a battle into a desire. The PR-before-product sequencing is the underrated move: awards recognition wasn't vanity, it was the fundraising and physician-recruitment instrument that made the product real.

## Steal
When your audience must endure a repeated, hated ritual, don't make the ritual more bearable — fuse it with something they already crave until the treatment and the pleasure are the same physical act. And if your product needs gatekeepers (regulators, physicians, investors) before it can exist, treat earned fame as infrastructure: launch the idea to win credibility before you launch the product to win customers.
