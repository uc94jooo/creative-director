# Phone Break (KitKat · 2025)

## Insight
The average person now spends almost 4 hours a day on a smartphone, and research shows 91% of people feel better after blocking the internet from their phones for two weeks. Donald Gilles wrote "Have a break, have a KitKat" in 1957 as a call to unwind — and a world glued to screens, incapable of taking a proper break, has made a 70-year-old tagline more relevant than it has ever been. As CCO Jake Barrow put it: as a species, we all look a bit silly sometimes.

## Mechanic
An out-of-home and print campaign in which KitKat bars replace smartphones in instantly recognisable screentime postures — hunched over a bar while waiting for the bus, staring at a bar across the table from a friend, scrolling a bar in a queue. The iconic tagline is invoked without being shown: the behaviour itself, plus the bar in the hand, does all the work. The concept won a VML global internal pitch that attracted over 400 submissions, and ran through April 2025 across billboards, subway stations, and print.

## Why it worked
P03 (Behavior Inversion): the campaign swaps the object of compulsion (phone) for the object of pause (KitKat), making the audience see their own habit from outside — the joke lands because viewers recognise themselves mid-scroll. P07 (Invisible Brand, applied to the asset): withholding one of advertising's most famous taglines and letting the audience complete it is a confidence move only a brand with 70 years of equity can make — the recall work happens in the viewer's head. The craft is minimal and the observation is universal, which is exactly what a static medium rewards.

## Steal
If your brand asset is famous enough, test the "complete it yourself" move: stage the behaviour your tagline answers and delete the tagline. An audience that finishes the sentence has engaged more deeply than one that merely read it.
