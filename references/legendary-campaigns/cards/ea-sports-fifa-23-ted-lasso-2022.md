# FIFA 23 x Ted Lasso (EA Sports & Apple · 2022)

## Insight
FIFA 23 sells itself on authenticity — 700+ real clubs, getting more "REAL" every release. Ted Lasso's marketing had always played the same game in reverse: treating fictional AFC Richmond as a real club, with in-world Twitter accounts and bespoke post-match interviews for matches that only happened in the show. Two entertainment properties, both obsessed with blurring fiction and reality, could validate each other: the most authentic place a "real" team could ever show up is inside the world's biggest sports video game.

## Mechanic
AFC Richmond became the first ever completely fictional football team playable in FIFA — Ted Lasso and every squad member rendered as playable characters, with player rating stats developed alongside the show's writers to match each character's playing style and story arc. Crucially, there was no show branding anywhere: the partnership launched not with a press release but with a teaser on Ted's in-world Twitter account showing the back of his head in a 360° motion-capture scan, igniting fan speculation before a launch film confirmed it the next day (7M views in 24 hours). Released globally September 20, 2022 with zero paid media. Results: 13.2bn earned impressions in 30 days, 2,000+ articles, AFC Richmond became the 17th most-played team out of 700+ real clubs, and Ted Lasso streaming on Apple TV+ rose 35%.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): rather than advertising the show inside the game, the partnership hijacked FIFA's core equity — authenticity — and made the intrusion of fiction into it the story itself. P07 (Invisible Brand): the deliberate absence of any show branding was the credibility mechanism; for gamers to accept a fictional team, it had to behave exactly like a real one, so the marketing wore the game's own conventions (motion-capture scans, player ratings, roster reveals) instead of ad language. The fandom did the media work: seeding through an in-world account converted announcement into mystery, and mystery into 13.2bn earned impressions — more social buzz than the show's four Emmy wins generated.

## Steal
When two properties share the same underlying game (here: pretending fiction is real), merge their worlds instead of cross-promoting them. And when entering a community that despises sponsorship, strip the branding entirely and follow the community's native rituals — the absence of ad language is what makes the placement feel earned.
