# Three Words (AXA France · 2024)

## Insight
Reported domestic violence cases in France doubled between 2016 and 2023 — from 124,000 to over 270,000, with actual cases estimated near one million a year. The question survivors hear is always "why does she stay?" — and the answer in their own words is brutal: "I stayed because I didn't have anywhere to go." French home insurance is mandatory by law and already relocates families within 24 hours when a home becomes uninhabitable by fire or flood. The insight: for a victim of domestic violence, home is uninhabitable too. The entire solution already existed inside the policy — it was three words short.

## Mechanic
AXA, France's leading insurer, amended its home insurance policies to add "and domestic violence" ("et violences conjugales") to the list of covered risks. Victims and their children now get emergency rehousing within 24 hours — AXA's existing disaster-recovery muscle repointed — with coverage for seven renewable days, plus legal, psychological and financial support through AXA's legal subsidiary Juridica, a dedicated legal helpline, and a 24/7 assistance unit arranging safe, anonymous accommodation and transportation. The guarantee costs policyholders nothing extra and applies even when the contract isn't in the victim's name. Publicis Conseil built a national campaign — TV, billboards, press, digital, social, an explainer film and a documentary — centered on the three added words, with plans to expand the model beyond France.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): the idea lives in the policy document, not the advertising — the ad merely shows a contract edit that is legally real. P13 (Truth Telling): it names the unspoken reason women stay (nowhere to go, no money, no resources) and answers it materially instead of sympathetically. P04 (Brand as Activist): an insurer reclassifying domestic violence as a covered peril changes what the entire category defines as "risk," pressuring the industry to follow. The creative economy is extraordinary: three words of copy unlock an existing 24-hour national logistics system for a million women — the smallest possible edit with the largest possible consequence.

## Steal
Before inventing new infrastructure for a social problem, audit the infrastructure your product already operates — then find the definitional exclusion that keeps the problem outside it. Often the bravest creative act is an edit to your own terms and conditions, and the campaign is simply publishing it.
