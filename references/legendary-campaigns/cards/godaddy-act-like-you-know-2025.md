# Act Like You Know (GoDaddy · 2025)

## Insight
Every small-business owner fakes competence they don't have — pricing, legal, marketing, a website — because starting a business has always required knowing things nobody actually knows going in. GoDaddy's new AI tool Airo does the "knowing" for them; the campaign only had to prove it, not claim it.

## Mechanic
In November 2024, Emmy-nominated actor Walton Goggins launched Goggins Goggles — a real, buyable eyewear line with an e-commerce site built entirely using GoDaddy's AI-powered small-business tool, Airo. The campaign built to a Super Bowl spot, "Act Like You Know," aired February 9, 2025: Goggins, playing on his own reputation for disappearing into convincing characters, confesses he has no idea what he's actually doing running the business — "but GoDaddy Airo does." The brand exists to fake competence, and Airo is real enough that the star didn't have to fake using it.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): instead of a celebrity endorsement reciting features, Goggins actually ran a functioning business built on Airo months before the ad aired — the case study was the campaign. P17 (Design as Idea): casting an actor famous for total character immersion made "acting like you know" the perfect metaphor for what every first-time founder does, collapsing the gap between Goggins' craft and the small-business owner's daily reality. Grand Prix jury cited "boldness, confident execution, and pure B2B creativity" for a category that rarely produces award-level craft.

## Steal
When your product does something customers are embarrassed to admit they can't do themselves, don't advertise the feature — stage a real, verifiable use of it by someone whose whole public persona is convincingly faking competence, then let the audience discover the fakery was real.
