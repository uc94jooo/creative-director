# In Love We Trust (Sinyi Realty · 2020)

## Insight
Taiwan's divorce rate ranks first in Asia and second in the world, and in Taiwanese culture "marriage" carries the literal meaning of "building a home" — so a generation afraid of commitment is, for a realtor, a generation that stops buying houses. Sinyi Realty, the market leader, had a genuine business stake in the country's fear of marriage. The emotional insight: young people don't doubt love, they doubt permanence — because they only ever see the endings.

## Mechanic
Sinyi released a long-form brand film set in Taiwan's household registration office — the single government counter that registers birth, death, marriage and divorce. A young clerk there witnesses life's cruelest separations daily, counting marriages against divorces until she loses faith and stops noticing the numbers. Then she meets an old man who lost his wife but keeps her name in his heart forever — proof of an unchanging love that outlasts separation — and finds the courage to accept her boyfriend's proposal. The launch was engineered in three waves: first a new coined term, "The Age of Doubting Marriage," seeded to dominate Google search and news; then the film dropped into the live "to marry or not to marry" debate; then KOLs and celebrities re-ignited the discussion. Within a week: 14 million views, 4 million likes, 4,400 shares, 40+ media pickups, and the most shared ad in Taiwan for 2020.

## Why it worked
P16 (Long-form Drama): the film earns its emotional turn by sitting inside real bureaucratic mundanity — the registration office is the one setting where love's statistics and love's endurance share a counter. P13 (Truth Telling): rather than selling houses, the brand names the real cultural anxiety blocking its category and answers it honestly, positioning agents as "selling hope, not just houses." The strategy is also a masterclass in conversation design: coining the term before the film gave media a headline and netizens a label for a feeling they already had, so the film arrived as the answer to a debate the brand itself had framed.

## Steal
Find the upstream life decision that feeds your category — marriage before mortgages, babies before minivans — and address the fear blocking that decision instead of advertising the product downstream of it. And when launching emotional work, name the cultural tension first: give the press the noun, then release the film as its resolution.
