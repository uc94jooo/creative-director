# Addresspollution.org (Central Office of Public Interest · 2021)

## Insight
Air pollution kills 10,000 Londoners prematurely every year — a coroner ruled it contributed to the death of nine-year-old Ella Adoo-Kissi-Debrah — yet it stays ignored because it's invisible and the data feels irrelevant to daily life. But there is one thing nobody in the UK ignores: house prices. Tie the invisible killer to property value and you convert public apathy into homeowner self-interest — and recruit the country's most politically connected people as a lobbying force.

## Mechanic
Working with King's College London, COPI mapped 1.5 billion data points accurate to 20 square metres and built a five-tier air quality rating for every address in the UK, published free at addresspollution.org. A commissioned study linked illegal pollution levels to property value impacts of up to a fifth. The launch infiltrated the property industry: spiky billboards in wealthy, polluted areas like Chelsea; ratings projected onto London's most expensive residential buildings; 650 real-time DOOH placements that activated during pollution spikes; direct mail warning estate agents of their legal obligation to disclose material health information. Over 465,000 London households generated reports — more than double annual property transactions. Zoopla adopted the system, estate agents became legally obliged to disclose the data, councils adopted petitioned measures, and the government brought forward the petrol and diesel car ban.

## Why it worked
P13 (Truth Telling): the campaign didn't create new information, it made suppressed public data unavoidable — address by address, name by name. P05 (Cultural Hijack): it hijacked Britain's property obsession, riding the one national conversation with guaranteed attention and attaching a public health crisis to it. The genius was targeting: polluted postcodes were also the richest ones, so the people warned were exactly the people with a direct line to Westminster. Self-interest did the lobbying that altruism never would. Systemic endgame — legal disclosure obligations — meant the idea couldn't be un-run. Cannes Grand Prix 2020/2021.

## Steal
When your issue is invisible or abstract, don't ask people to care about it — attach it to something they already can't stop caring about (their home, their money, their kids' school). Make the data personal to a specific address or name, then aim the discomfort at the audience with the most power to change policy, not the most sympathy.
