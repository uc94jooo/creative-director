# Knock Knock (Korean National Police Agency · 2022)

## Insight
Domestic violence in South Korea rose 718% in eight years, yet only 2% of cases are reported. The reason is structural, not attitudinal: victims can't speak because they are in the same room as their abuser. Every emergency system in the world assumes the caller can talk. So the question became: how can the police help a victim who cannot say a word?

## Mechanic
Inspired by Morse code, Cheil and the Korean National Police Agency created a voiceless emergency protocol. A victim dials 112 (Korea's 911) and simply taps any number twice. The call handler recognises the signal and sends a link that lets police monitor the situation through the caller's camera, track their location without a formal LBS request, and chat covertly through an interface disguised as a Google search page so the abuser notices nothing — enabling immediate, precise intervention. The protocol was seeded where victims actually are: beauty channels, nail and hair salons, and other spaces frequented by women, plus OOH at police stations and government offices. It also serves the deaf community — deaf actress So Byul Lee endorsed the campaign — and 4,800 call handlers nationwide were trained to respond.

## Why it worked
P06 (Limitation as Power): the victim's inability to speak — the exact barrier that suppressed reporting — becomes the interface itself; two taps is a language anyone can use under surveillance. P18 (Tech as Canvas): no new app to download, no infrastructure to build — the system runs on any phone and hides inside the most ordinary screen on earth, a search page, which is precisely what makes it safe. The proof of the idea is institutional adoption: 5,749 emergency links sent, and 'Knock Knock' formally recognised as an official 112 emergency call in South Korea, with 237M impressions and national news coverage. Glass Grand Prix at Cannes 2023.

## Steal
Design for the user's most constrained moment, not the average one. If your audience cannot speak, type, or be seen using your service, the service must disguise itself as something mundane — invisibility to the wrong observer is a feature, and the strongest campaign outcome is becoming official infrastructure.
