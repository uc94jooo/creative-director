# Eternal Run (ASICS · 2019)

## Insight
ASICS was losing the performance-running crown to brands previously known for fashion, and needed to launch the GLIDERIDE — a shoe engineered to save energy as you run. The strategic reframe: the sports world is obsessed with finish lines, but what if the finish line itself is what holds athletes back from knowing what their minds and bodies are truly capable of? For a brand whose name is an acronym for "a sound mind in a sound body," removing the finish line was both a product demo and a philosophy made physical.

## Mechanic
Eternal Run: the world's first race without a finish line, staged on the Bonneville Salt Flats in Utah. 23 athletes, influencers and journalists were each assigned a personal pace — calculated by sport scientist Professor Samuele Marcora, RUNKEEPER data analysts, elite coaches and the ASICS Institute of Sport Science. When the whistle blew, they ran at that pace until they couldn't: drop below pace and you got a warning; three strikes and your race was over. No pacemakers, no distance target — a pure duel against your own perceived limits, in the energy-saving shoe. Social content shipped within hours of the race; short films and a 10-minute documentary carried it to wider audiences, with journalist-participants securing major titles. Earned reach: 1.74 billion.

## Why it worked
P03 (Behavior Inversion): every race in history is defined by its end; deleting the finish line inverted the category's most fundamental convention and made the format itself newsworthy. P12 (Social Experiment): "how far can you go when nothing stops you?" is a testable human question, and the runners' results (most outran their predicted limits) became the story. P14 (Product as Proof): an energy-saving shoe demands an endurance format — the mechanic is a live product test, not a metaphor. Casting journalists and influencers as participants, not spectators, built the earned engine into the event itself.

## Steal
Find your category's most sacred convention — the element every competitor treats as immovable — and delete it. Then make sure the people you'd normally pitch coverage to are inside the experience: a journalist who ran the race writes a different story than one who watched it.
