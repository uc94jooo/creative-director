## Insight
Carnival creates two simultaneous, opposing pressure points in Brazilian cities: tourists face sky-high short-term rents, and brands face oversaturated, expensive out-of-home ad inventory during the one week everyone is actually looking at the street. Both problems dissolve into one if a beer brand pays part of what the tourist would already owe, in exchange for turning the balcony they're renting into ad space.

## Mechanic
Brahma partnered with Airbnb-style hosts across five Brazilian cities during Carnival 2025, offering tourists a rent discount in exchange for displaying a Brahma-branded banner on their balcony for the duration of their stay — over 2,350 banners went up, each customized to fit local regulations and building architecture. In practice, the discount was modest: in Salvador, the best-documented case, hosts received roughly 7.5% of the average local rent, not the ~50% figure claimed in the original Cannes submission.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): Carnival is a week when the entire built environment of a city already becomes decoration and spectacle — turning balconies into ad space extends a behavior residents and tourists already expect during Carnival, rather than imposing an unwanted intrusion. P06 (Limitation as Power): the category's real constraint — OOH inventory during Carnival is scarce and overpriced — became the resource itself, sourced directly from the people already renting out their homes. The mechanic (a real transaction between brand and host) is structurally sound and repeatable even at the verified, much smaller discount — the design didn't need the inflated number to function; only the award submission did.

## Steal
When your category's ad inventory during a high-demand cultural moment is scarce and overpriced, look for an adjacent market (short-term housing, event tickets, parking) already transacting in that same physical space, and offer a modest, real discount for ad placement rather than paying inflated OOH rates. Whatever number you use to describe the offer's impact in a case study or award submission, verify it independently before publishing — a real, modest, and structurally sound idea does not need an inflated headline number to justify itself.
