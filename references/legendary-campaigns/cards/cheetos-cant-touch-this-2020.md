# Can't Touch This (Cheetos · 2020)

## Insight
Cheetos had to launch Cheetos Popcorn into a subcategory that stood for everything it wasn't: SkinnyPop and BOOMCHICKAPOP sold "simple, healthy, wholesome, on-the-go" — while Cheetos is messy, sticky, and overwhelmingly orange. But 56% of popcorn buyers shared the "Rejuvenile" mindset — life's practical jokers, whom no popcorn brand was talking to. And the thing Rejuveniles loved most about Cheetos was precisely its biggest product flaw: messy cheese-dust fingertips, which get you out of boring, serious things like work and chores. The weakness was the aspiration.

## Mechanic
GS&P named the dust "Cheetle" and turned it into a superpower. Returning to the Super Bowl for the first time in ten years, Cheetos teased that Cheetle was the true inspiration behind MC Hammer's "U Can't Touch This," then aired a game-day spot in which the hero's Cheetle-covered fingers become the perfect excuse to get out of everything — he literally can't touch this. An integrated social campaign officially named and claimed the term Cheetle, deployed mascot Chester, and launched a Cheetle finger detector unlocking exclusive digital content.

## Why it worked
P06 (Limitation as Power): the category-disqualifying flaw (mess) was flipped into the entire creative platform — you cannot out-clean SkinnyPop, but you can own dirty. P05 (Cultural Hijack): borrowing MC Hammer's thirty-year-old catchphrase gave the joke instant, pre-loaded recognition on the Super Bowl stage. P15 (Benefit Hyperbole): "dust as superpower" exaggerated a tiny product truth into a fantasy of escaping adulthood. Sales hit $100M against a $40M forecast (250% to plan), the best Frito-Lay innovation of 2020, driving 75% of ready-to-eat popcorn growth and adding 1.75M new households (+1.4pt penetration). Cannes Grand Prix 2020/2021.

## Steal
List the product attributes your category treats as disqualifying, then ask which one your heaviest users secretly love. Flip that into the hero benefit and hyperbolize it. Naming the flaw (Cheetle) converts an embarrassment into ownable brand property no competitor can touch.
