# Child Wedding Cards (UN Women · 2022)

## Insight
Complications from pregnancy and childbirth are among the leading causes of death for girls aged 15–19, yet child marriage persists in Pakistan because the law has not moved. Mass awareness campaigns don't reach the only audience that can change that: lawmakers. In Pakistani culture, the hand-delivered printed wedding card carries enormous social weight — it is considered offensive NOT to receive one. That ritual object could carry the message straight into the National Assembly, one-on-one, in a form no member could ignore.

## Mechanic
UN Women invited dozens of girls aged 5 to 15 — including an actual child bride — to freely draw and sketch. Their drawings were combined into six wedding card designs, each reflecting one region of the country, formatted exactly like the millions of real wedding invitations printed in Pakistan every year. Members of the National Assembly then received a personal invitation to a fictional child's wedding, designed entirely by children. A short film and social posts accompanied the direct mail drop. The card turned each recipient into a messenger: lawmakers gave public statements holding the cards, and in an actual parliamentary session members held up the Child Wedding Cards to demand the minimum marriageable age be raised to 18. The Federal Islamic Court subsequently announced a landmark edict that 18 is applicable as the minimum age for marriage in Islam.

## Why it worked
P01 (Format as Idea): the medium is the entire idea — a wedding invitation for a wedding that should never happen, delivered through a channel culturally impossible to ignore. P17 (Design as Idea): the children's own drawings do the persuasion; craft carries the horror precisely because it looks innocent. P13 (Truth Telling): the card confronts each lawmaker personally with a truth usually kept statistical and distant. The secondary objective was structural: rather than the NGO spreading the message, the targets themselves became ambassadors, holding the artifact up on camera and in parliament.

## Steal
When your real audience is a handful of decision-makers rather than the public, don't buy reach — design a single unignorable object and put it in their hands through a ritual channel they cannot refuse. Let the artifact be damning enough that holding it makes them part of the campaign.
