# Runner 321 (Adidas · 2022)

## Insight
When people with Down syndrome don't see themselves represented in sport, they can't see what's possible for them — separate competitions quietly signal that neurodivergent athletes don't belong in the mainstream. Meanwhile, sport's most powerful shorthand for greatness is the iconic number: Jordan's 23, Messi's 10, Gretzky's 99. Those numbers have only ever represented athletes who fit inside mainstream sport. Nobody had ever created an iconic number for a community.

## Mechanic
Adidas created Runner 321 — a movement asking the world's major marathons to reserve bib number 321 for a neurodivergent athlete who qualifies. The number stands for Trisomy 21, the chromosome that causes Down syndrome. Launched on World Down Syndrome Day (March 21, 2022) with Chris Nikic — the first athlete with Down syndrome to complete an Ironman and the first globally sponsored athlete with Down syndrome — the campaign offered every race a toolkit with instructions and social assets. Activations included a takeover of the Fifth Avenue Adidas store and cheering sections at mile 3.21 of the NYC and Boston Marathons; after finishing Boston, Chris handed his bib to the next Runner 321, Kayleigh Williamson, seeding a relay ritual. All six major marathons (Boston, New York, Tokyo, London, Berlin, Chicago) reserved bib 321 — despite four being sponsored by Nike, New Balance and ASICS. 252 races committed, 278 athletes signed up, 268M earned impressions. Direct Grand Prix, Cannes 2023.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): it commandeers sport's existing status symbol — the iconic number — and assigns it to a community instead of an individual, so the meaning arrives instantly to any sports fan. P04 (Brand as Activist): Adidas built an open standard generous enough that its fiercest competitors adopted it inside their own sponsored races — the rare purpose play where rival participation strengthens the author. P10 (Serialization & Ritual): a reserved bib and a hand-off ceremony make the idea self-renewing every race season, not a one-time gesture. The mechanic costs races almost nothing (one bib) while the symbolism is permanent — a near-zero-friction ask with compounding cultural return.

## Steal
To change who belongs in a system, don't build a parallel system — reserve a permanent, symbolic slot inside the existing one. Make the ask so small (one number, one seat, one slot) that no gatekeeper can justify refusing, and design a hand-off ritual so the idea renews itself without you.
