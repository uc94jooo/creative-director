# The Best Place in the World to Have Herpes (NZ Herpes Foundation · 2023)

## Insight
Up to 80% of New Zealanders will contract herpes, yet 30% of diagnosed people report depressive or suicidal thoughts — driven not by the manageable condition itself but by decades of pop-culture punchlines that turned it into shame. The Foundation was drowning in distress calls with almost no budget. Two Kiwi truths unlocked it: humor is New Zealand's proven way into uncomfortable conversations, and at a moment of low national pride, Kiwis will rally behind a quirky patriotic mission. So destigmatising herpes was reframed as something New Zealand could be the best in the world at.

## Mechanic
Motion Sickness branded it as the world's most unlikely tourism campaign: positioning New Zealand as "The Best Place in the World to Have Herpes." Nostalgic tourism-style content stacked iconic Kiwi imagery and recruited national hero Sir Graham Henry (former All Blacks coach) as frontman. A live global Herpes Stigma Index ranked countries, and the world's first "Herpes Destigmatisation Course" gamified education — Kiwis earned leaderboard points for the nation by completing educational content, turning stigma reduction into a collective, pride-driven sport. Multi-channel storytelling and PR carried it globally on an NZ$80,000 budget.

## Why it worked
P11 (Absurd as Carrier): "a tourism campaign for herpes" is an absurd frame that makes an unspeakable topic instantly speakable — the joke does the destigmatising before the education arrives. P05 (Cultural Hijack): it hijacked both the tourism-campaign format and national pride itself, converting patriotism into participation. P03 (Behavior Inversion): the campaign inverted shame into competitive pride — instead of hiding a diagnosis, the nation competed to be best at not caring. Results: 10,776 hours of content viewed in 8 weeks (a full year's worth of herpes education), 22M+ global PR impressions from 100+ stories, 86% of participants more comfortable discussing herpes, and plans to embed the course in NZ high schools. Cannes Grand Prix winner (Health & Wellness, 2024).

## Steal
When stigma is the real disease, don't fight it with earnestness — the audience flinches before the facts land. Wrap the taboo in a familiar, proudly absurd format (tourism ad, index, leaderboard) that gives people a socially safe, even fun, reason to engage. Gamified collective identity ("do it for the country") converts private shame into public participation at almost zero media cost.
