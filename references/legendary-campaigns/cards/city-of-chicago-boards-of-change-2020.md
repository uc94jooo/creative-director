# Boards of Change (City of Chicago · 2020)

## Insight
After George Floyd's murder, righteous anger brought millions into the streets — but decades of disenfranchisement and voter suppression left many Black Americans feeling their vote didn't count, and one in five eligible Americans weren't even registered. During the unrest, stores barricaded windows with plywood, and those boards became canvases where the community painted its frustration and hope. The boards held the exact energy the ballot box lacked: people smashed windows because they felt they had no voice — so give the boards a voice that counts.

## Mechanic
The City of Chicago, with Mayor Lori Lightfoot and Michelle Obama's When We All Vote, transformed the painted plywood boards — symbols of destruction — into working voting booths. The collection toured dozens of Chicago's Black and Brown neighborhoods as a traveling exhibition, and each booth enabled the crucial first step, voter registration, via a simple smartphone scan. PR was built around real protagonists: the mayor launching with a fiery speech at the boards, the artists who painted them, store owners, community members, and the Chicago Board of Elections; dozens of respected Black Chicagoan influencers amplified it. Coverage ran from the Chicago Tribune front page to NBC Nightly News; Chicago hit a record number of registrations and 73% turnout, and the boards now live at the DuSable Museum of African American History.

## Why it worked
P03 (Behavior Inversion): the object that symbolized broken democracy became the instrument of democracy — the transformation itself was the message, requiring no copywriting. P17 (Design as Idea): the physical artifact carried the entire idea; press coverage was guaranteed because the object was the story. P04 (Brand as Activist): a city government acted like a movement, channeling street energy "from the streets to the polls" with an authentic, cost-efficient gesture rather than a PSA. The emotional register — strength claimed from acknowledged powerlessness — is exactly what made it land as empowerment rather than lecture. Cannes Grand Prix 2020/2021.

## Steal
Find the artifact your audience already invested with emotion — especially a negative or painful one — and physically convert it into the tool that resolves the emotion. Repurposed symbols outperform new ones because the audience did the meaning-making for you. Then let the people who made the symbol (artists, owners, victims) front the story.
