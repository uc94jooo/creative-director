# Notpla (Notpla · 2019)

## Insight
Every year 8 million tonnes of plastic are dumped in the oceans, and consumers have become not just aware but emotionally connected to the problem. A start-up had invented a revolutionary seaweed-based material that decomposes naturally in weeks (versus 700 years for plastic) — but unlike bioplastics, which behave like and technically still are plastics, it had fully abandoned synthetic materials. The branding insight: it's not common practice to define a brand by what it isn't — but when what it isn't is one of the world's biggest problems, that negation is the strongest possible claim.

## Mechanic
Superunion named the material and the company "Notpla" — an abbreviation of "Not Plastic" — making the brand name a category rejection and a material specification in one word. The identity centers on the mission "making packaging disappear": an animated, interactive logo behaves like a vessel that fills with water, juice, or sauce, and when emptied, its exterior disappears — just like the packaging itself. The tone of voice is simple, pared back, and bold, refusing the overcomplication typical of sustainability brands. The brand was activated through the redesigned website and real-world trials: 36,000+ edible Ooho capsules consumed at the London Marathon (cutting carbon emissions 80% per unit versus plastic cups), partnerships with Just Eat and Unilever that kept 46,000+ plastic ketchup sachets out of landfill, and 400,000 sachets produced — with 92% of customers asking to see more.

## Why it worked
P17 (Design as Idea): the name *is* the strategy — no tagline or manifesto needed, because "Notpla" states the entire reason to believe in six letters. The disappearing logo extends the product truth into brand behavior. P02 (Enemy or Conflict): the brand is structurally defined against a named enemy (plastic), giving every appearance built-in tension and news value. P14 (Product as Proof): marathon capsules and ketchup sachets in the wild proved the claim in consumers' hands — the demos were the media. For a start-up with no ad budget, packing positioning, product, and mission into the name itself was maximum leverage per dollar.

## Steal
If your product's defining virtue is the absence of something bad, consider naming the negation — a name that states the enemy does positioning work forever, for free. Then make the identity system behave like the product (a logo that disappears, a material that vanishes): when brand behavior mirrors product truth, every touchpoint compounds the claim.
