# Aeronaut VR (William Patrick Corgan · 2018)

## Insight
In the digital economy, consumer expectations of creative experiences had risen to an all-time high — a conventional music video or tour promo no longer registers. To launch Ogilala, Billy Corgan's (William Patrick Corgan of the Smashing Pumpkins) first solo album in over a decade, he needed an experience that would contemporise his work and articulate his artistic vision, not just distribute a single. The album launch itself had to demonstrate the artistry it was promoting.

## Mechanic
Isobar, with directors David Liu and Rob Ruffler and Viacom NEXT, created "Aeronaut": a hologram of Corgan performing, captured as volumetric video at Microsoft's Mixed Reality Capture Studios, then imported into an animated dreamworld hand-built by VR artist Danny Bittman using Google's Tilt Brush and Blocks. The result was billed as a world-first in VR — a uniquely immersive and social music experience where the audience shares space with the artist's holographic performance. The piece was then edited in Unity and rendered into a 2D film, doubling as the teaser music video for the VR experience. In 2018 Aeronaut won the Grand Prix for Digital Craft at Cannes Lions, and Isobar was named to Fast Company's Top 10 VR/AR Companies.

## Why it worked
P18 (Tech as Canvas): the technology stack — volumetric capture, Tilt Brush world-building, Unity — isn't a gimmick bolted onto a music video; it is the artistic medium itself, chosen because it embodies the album's intimate, otherworldly register. P08 (Craft as Message): every layer is visibly hand-crafted (a painted VR world, a captured live performance), which repositions a legacy artist as a frontier one — the craft is the proof of contemporary relevance. The 2D render solved VR's classic reach problem: the headset experience creates the story, the flat film distributes it.

## Steal
When launching a legacy brand or artist to a new generation, don't advertise the comeback — build the work in a frontier medium so the format itself makes the relevance argument. And always design the low-fidelity distributable version (2D capture of the 3D experience) so the exclusive experience can still travel at scale.
