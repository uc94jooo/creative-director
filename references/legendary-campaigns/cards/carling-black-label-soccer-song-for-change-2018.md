# Soccer Song for Change (Carling Black Label · 2018)

## Insight
South Africa has one of the world's highest rates of violence against women — roughly three deaths a day — and there is an acute, acknowledged link between men who abuse alcohol and men who abuse women. Carling Black Label, the beer brand most deeply woven into South African football culture, sat inside the problem. The songs men sing in stadiums — traditional, communal, often sexist — are the soundtrack of that culture. You cannot ban a song a hundred thousand men know by heart. But you can change what it says.

## Mechanic
For the Soweto Derby, one of the most important fixtures in South African football, the campaign took traditional soccer songs and rewrote their lyrics into messages of respect and safety toward women — then had fans themselves sing the new versions inside the stadium. Instead of lecturing men from outside their culture, the campaign hijacked the ritual from within: same melodies, same collective voice, new words. The moment was broadcast and amplified nationally, positioning it explicitly as "not a campaign for men — a societal campaign," delivered by the brand those men already drink.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): it commandeered an existing cultural asset — the stadium song — rather than inventing a new one, so the message arrived pre-loaded with emotional ownership. P04 (Brand as Activist): an alcohol brand naming the alcohol-violence link about its own consumers is a costly, credible signal, not borrowed virtue. P09 (User as Co-Author): the men who were the target of the behaviour change were also the performers of the message — you cannot dismiss a message you are singing. It won the Radio & Audio Grand Prix at Cannes Lions 2018 because the medium (the crowd itself) was the idea.

## Steal
When you need to change behaviour inside a tight culture, don't import a new message vehicle — rewrite one the culture already sings, chants, or repeats. And if your brand is part of the problem, saying so out loud is the fastest route to permission to speak.
