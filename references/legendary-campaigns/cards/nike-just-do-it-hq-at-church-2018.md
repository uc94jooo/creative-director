# Just Do It HQ at the Church (Nike · 2018)

## Insight
For the 30th anniversary of "Just Do It," Nike wanted new generations of athletes to enter the chase for greatness — not by watching another ad, but by getting access. In Chicago's most underserved neighborhoods, access to organized sport is critical and scarce, and sport can be both a safe haven and a catalyst for change for the city's youth. The brand's philosophy would mean nothing to these kids unless it was built as a physical place they could walk into.

## Mechanic
In partnership with the community, Nike Chicago transformed the vacant 19th-century Church of Epiphany in the West Loop into a cultural hub for basketball and the ultimate summer training program. The design honored the existing structural elements and borrowed the church's architecture and color palette, amplifying them with modern sports-centric materials: a full-length court under the nave, lockers, a weight and trainers' room, and free Nike gear. Open for four weeks, the space offered training for young athletes, interactive workshops, skills clinics, and access to Chicago sports legends from the NBA, WNBA, and NCAA — helping Chicagoland youth chase their dreams on and off the court.

## Why it worked
P17 (Design as Idea): the sacred-to-sport transformation is the message — a church literally reborn as a basketball sanctuary makes "Just Do It" a place of worship without saying a word. The art direction carried the strategy: aspiration through elevated aesthetics, not signage. P04 (Brand as Activist): instead of celebrating the anniversary with retrospective films, Nike spent it on infrastructure for kids who lack access to sport, converting a brand birthday into community equity. The tension between reverence (19th-century architecture) and street energy (Chicago hoops) gave media and visitors an image they couldn't ignore.

## Steal
When your brand hits a milestone, resist the retrospective film. Ask instead: what physical space could make our philosophy literal for the audience that needs it most? Repurposing a loaded existing space (church, bank, prison) imports its symbolic weight for free — design the transformation so the old meaning amplifies the new one.
