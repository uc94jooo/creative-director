# Anne de Gaulle (Fondation Anne de Gaulle · 2022)

## Insight
Everyone in France knows Charles de Gaulle — his name is on streets, stations, and the largest airport in Europe. Almost nobody knows Anne de Gaulle, his daughter born with Down syndrome, in whose honour he and Yvonne created a foundation in 1945 to fight for the inclusion of the mentally disabled. That asymmetry of visibility was the insight itself: 12 million French people are officially disabled yet remain largely invisible — notably in transport — just as Anne is invisible inside her own father's monumental name. Making one name visible could make twelve million people impossible to ignore.

## Mechanic
For one week around International Day of Persons with Disabilities (December 3–11), Paris-Charles de Gaulle Airport — the largest public infrastructure in France, with direct lineage to the foundation's story — was renamed Paris-Anne de Gaulle. The rebrand was total: gigantic letters on the terminal façade, luggage tags, carts, queue barriers, boarding passes, every flight information display and airport TV screen, baggage reclaim displays, even the road signage around the airport. 1.4 million travellers passed through during the week, immersed in an experience that made mental disability unavoidable. The first-ever name change of a major international airport for a cause generated 200+ articles in 13 countries, €1M+ in earned media, 50M reach, forced a response from the French transport minister, and pushed operator Groupe ADP to kick-start its accessibility transformation ahead of the Paris 2024 Olympic and Paralympic Games.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): the campaign hijacked one of France's most sacred cultural assets — the de Gaulle name — but with unimpeachable legitimacy, since the foundation was created by de Gaulle himself; the hijack was really a restoration, which converted potential controversy into national poignancy. P17 (Design as Idea): the idea is literally a renaming — a design/identity change carried through every physical and digital touchpoint. The scale was the argument: a small foundation borrowed the country's biggest infrastructure to make its cause proportionally big, and the one-week limit created news urgency while the operator's lasting accessibility commitments turned a stunt into structural change.

## Steal
Look for the founder's story, forgotten name, or buried lineage inside an institution — legitimacy you can inherit rather than manufacture. Then express the cause as a single, total identity change across every touchpoint of a massive public space: renaming something everyone already uses forces the conversation into everyone's day.
