# Creatability (Google · 2018)

## Insight
More than 56 million people live with a disability, and creative tools — drawing, music-making — largely assume a specific body: ten fingers, sighted eyes, hearing ears. Meanwhile, history shows that accessibility research ends up making technology better for everyone (the typewriter, recorded sound). With browser-based AI like TensorFlow.js and PoseNet newly able to track the whole body through a webcam, Google saw the chance to prove that AI's benefits could be designed inclusively from the start — not adapted after the fact — by building with the accessibility community rather than for it.

## Mechanic
Creatability is a set of eight open-source web experiments, co-created with creators and allies from the accessibility community, exploring how creative tools can be made more accessible with web and AI technology. Each experiment accepts whatever input works best for the person — voice, body tracking, keyboard, mouse, stylus, switch devices — and designs outputs with equal care (spoken and sound feedback for visually impaired users). Examples: Sound Canvas lets a non-visual user draw with real-time audio feedback; Seeing Music lets a deaf composer experience music through the eyes; Keyboard/body-tracking instruments broaden a musical instrument from ten fingers to any part of the body. Everything runs in the browser via TensorFlow.js — anyone with internet, a browser and a webcam can click and instantly try it. The code was open-sourced with guides so others could build further, and the launch deliberately spotlighted the partners (NYU Ability Project, openup music, accessibility educators), not Google.

## Why it worked
P06 (Limitation as Power): the constraint of designing for every input and output didn't compromise the design — it produced simpler, better tools, proving the campaign's own thesis in its craft. P18 (Tech as Canvas): browser-native AI wasn't demonstrated with a tech demo but with human expression — the least "corporate AI" application imaginable. P09 (User as Co-Author): doubly so — the accessibility community co-authored the tools, and every user authors their own art and music with them; open-sourcing extended co-authorship to developers worldwide. For Google, it repositions AI from something done to people into something people create with, earning brand equity no product ad could buy.

## Steal
Design for the extreme user first — the constraints will force simpler, more universal solutions that serve everyone. And when doing purpose work in tech, hand the spotlight and the source code to the community you built with: their credibility and their extensions become the campaign's proof and its longevity.
