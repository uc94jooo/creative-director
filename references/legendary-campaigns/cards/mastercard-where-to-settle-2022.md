# Where To Settle (Mastercard · 2022)

## Insight
After the outbreak of war in Ukraine, almost 10 million refugees crossed into Poland — Europe's biggest migration crisis since WWII. Most crowded into major cities, driving rental hikes and job competition amid record inflation, while smaller Polish towns had housing and work to offer. In the first months, 34% of refugees couldn't decide where to live — and that indecision deepened their despair and delayed their independence. The missing ingredient wasn't shelter or goodwill; it was reliable cost-of-living data to make a life-defining decision.

## Mechanic
Mastercard built Where To Settle, a digital platform combining its anonymized spending data with Polish Central Statistical Office data. A refugee fills in an anonymous form — preferred employment, family size, needs — and the tool returns average costs of living, salaries, housing, and economic opportunities across Polish towns, surfacing where that specific family is most likely to thrive. Built with Morizon-Gratka (Poland's biggest housing/jobs listings group) and promoted by the Polish government and the Association of Polish Cities, with a social campaign of locals inviting refugees to their towns. 242K unique users, 73.7K completed forms — an estimated 20% of refugees in Poland used it — while Mastercard's usage intent among non-users hit 57% (Poles) and 80% (Ukrainians).

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): "financial inclusion" is Mastercard's stated purpose, and here the proof is infrastructural — the brand's transaction data becomes the product. P04 (Brand as Activist): rather than donating to the crisis, Mastercard deployed the one asset only it possessed (real spending data at city-level resolution) against the crisis's hardest practical question. It also aligned three interests at once — refugees needing homes, small towns needing workers, and a government needing to decongest cities — which is why institutions promoted it for free.

## Steal
In a crisis, audit what proprietary data your brand alone holds and ask what decision it could de-risk for the people affected. The strongest purpose work answers a practical question no one else can answer — utility first, message second.
