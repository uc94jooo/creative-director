# Raising Profiles (The Big Issue & LinkedIn · 2020)

## Insight
The Big Issue gives vulnerably housed people a legitimate income selling magazines on UK high streets. Lockdown took sales from 80,000 a week to zero — "we don't have an income if we don't have a street." The unlock: customers didn't buy the magazine, they bought from their vendor — the bond with a specific person on a specific corner. And those customers were office professionals, which meant roughly 70% of vendors' former customers were findable in one place: LinkedIn. The street corner could be rebuilt as a digital location.

## Mechanic
FCB Inferno and LinkedIn launched "Raising Profiles," a digital inclusion program that made LinkedIn the vendors' virtual street corner. Vendors received donated tablets (Dixons) and three weeks of training to set up profiles, post, comment and share. The media plan was pure reconstruction: cross-referencing workplace locations on LinkedIn against each vendor's old pitch recreated their former neighborhood digitally, reconnecting them with the same professionals who used to walk past them every day — people who might not know the vendor's name but knew them by sight. Each vendor carried a personalized e-commerce trigger, so any conversation could convert into a contact-free magazine subscription; every post effectively became an ad for The Big Issue. Zero media spend; PR and celebrity shares (Gary Lineker, Sadiq Khan, Dame Kelly Holmes) announced the pilot's 9 vendors to the world.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): the campaign hijacked LinkedIn's codes — the platform of CEOs and recruiters — for society's most excluded workers, and the friction of that collision generated the story. P03 (Behavior Inversion): vendors went from asking people to buy a magazine to sharing what's inside it — from soliciting to networking — which inverted public perception: "they're professionals with every right to be on LinkedIn." Results: LinkedIn drove 96% of subscription traffic (+325%), magazine sales rose 400%, conversion jumped from 1 sale per 50 street interactions to 1 per 10 online, 81.5M combined reach — and, per the Executive Director, a permanent perception shift in how people see vendors. Cannes Titanium/Grand Prix winner (2020/21).

## Steal
When a physical business loses its physical channel, don't digitize the product — digitize the relationship. Find where the existing customers already live online, rebuild the old geography there (workplace + location data is a map of past foot traffic), and give each frontline person their own commerce trigger. The status transfer is the multiplier: placing the excluded inside a high-status platform changes how they're seen, not just how they sell.
