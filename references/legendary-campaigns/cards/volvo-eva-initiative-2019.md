# The E.V.A. Initiative (Volvo · 2019)

## Insight
Most cars are designed for the average-sized man and tested mainly on male crash test dummies. The result: women are 71% more likely to be moderately injured, 47% more likely to be seriously injured, and 17% more likely to die in a car crash. Meanwhile 80% of car purchases are influenced by women, safety is their top priority — and 3 in 4 women feel misunderstood by the auto industry. Volvo alone had collected real-world collision data since the 1970s regardless of size or gender, making its cars equally safe for everyone. That archive was a competitive moat; giving it away would be a bigger idea than keeping it.

## Mechanic
Volvo collected 40+ years of safety research — data from more than 43,000 collisions and 72,000 people — into a digital library and made it free for anyone to download, explicitly including competitors, echoing Volvo's 1959 giveaway of the three-point seatbelt patent. E.V.A. (Equal Vehicles for All) launched at a live-streamed event journalists and rival brands could tune into, then rolled out globally across film, print, social, outdoor, and PR. To make the data human, the campaign "gave the numbers a face," showing how the injustice affects women personally; the print ad invited readers to literally put their hand on the issue. Results: 85M+ film views across 70+ countries, 450+ articles, 280M+ social impressions, competitors like EV startup Uniti embracing the initiative — and 11,000+ downloads of the research library.

## Why it worked
P13 (Truth Telling): the campaign exposed an industry-wide injustice most people had never heard of, with the courage to indict the category Volvo sits in. P14 (Product as Proof): the proof isn't a claim of safety — it's four decades of proprietary research, and the act of open-sourcing it demonstrates confidence no ad could fake. P04 (Brand as Activist): the ask ("download our data, competitors") converts brand purpose into an industry-level intervention, and the seatbelt-patent heritage makes the activism credible rather than opportunistic — the second time Volvo gave away safety. The tension between commercial logic (hoard the moat) and stated values (safety for all) resolving in favor of values is precisely what earned the coverage.

## Steal
Find the proprietary asset your brand hoards as competitive advantage and ask what would happen if you gave it away in the name of your purpose — the sacrifice itself is the story. Then humanize the dataset: statistics start conversations only after you give the numbers a face.
