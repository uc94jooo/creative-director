# Caption with Intention (Chicago Hearing Society · 2025)

## Insight
For 50 years, film has evolved — new cameras, CGI, immersive sound — but closed captions have remained unchanged since 1971. More than 430 million people with disabling hearing loss rely on them, yet captions aren't timed to the spoken word, don't convey tone or emotion, and don't identify who is speaking. Deaf audiences were reading movies while everyone else was feeling them.

## Mechanic
Over a year, FCB Chicago, the Chicago Hearing Society and Rakish Entertainment researched, prototyped and tested dozens of caption variations from iconic films with the Deaf community — in-person sessions, remote feedback, real-time design refinements. The result is a caption design system built on three moves: dynamic text animation synchronizes each word precisely as it's spoken; variable type sizes, weights and animation carry intonation, so you feel a voice get louder or softer; and character-specific colors identify who is speaking instantly. Demonstrated on films like Forrest Gump and Toy Story, the system was released open-source for adoption by studios and streaming platforms worldwide.

## Why it worked
P17 (Design as Idea): the creative act *is* a design system — typography itself becomes cinematic language, turning an accessibility utility into part of the film's craft. P08 (Craft as Message): every parameter (timing, weight, color) encodes meaning that words alone drop; Deaf viewers testing it reported following mood and story for the first time ("I've seen Forrest Gump many times, but now... I can feel so much more"). P06 (Limitation as Power): a constraint community (no audio) drove an innovation that arguably improves captions for everyone. Community co-creation gave it legitimacy; open-sourcing gave it a path to becoming an industry standard. The Academy recognized it with a Sci-Tech Award of Merit — an Oscar statuette, accepted by Marlee Matlin — and at Cannes 2025 it took a Titanium Lion plus three Grands Prix, the most awarded idea of the year.

## Steal
Find the interface element in your category that hasn't changed in decades and everyone tolerates — inertia that old is an innovation vacancy. Build it *with* the affected community, not for them, and release it open-source: giving the solution away is what converts a campaign into a standard, and a standard outlives any media flight.
