# Keeping Fortnite Fresh (Wendy's · 2018)

## Insight
Fortnite had become the cultural hub for 12- to 24-year-olds — the audience that eats QSR most often — but this audience creates entertainment for and by itself, avoids traditional advertising, and is hostile to brands invading its space. Other brands either tweeted from the sidelines or paid for in-game sponsorships; both marked them as outsiders. The only credible way in was to show up as a player, not an advertiser. The opening came from inside the game's own fiction: when Fortnite launched the "Food Fight" mode (Team Pizza vs. Team Burger), Wendy's discovered that the fictional Durr Burger chain stored its beef in freezers — directly at odds with Wendy's "fresh, never frozen" claim.

## Mechanic
On Nov. 29, 2018, Wendy's tweeted that it would stream Fortnite live on Twitch. Playing as a red-haired, pigtailed character, the brand joined Team Pizza and — instead of eliminating other players, the entire point of the game — spent nine hours straight hunting down and destroying every burger freezer at Durr Burger locations. The declared mission drove hundreds of thousands of gamers to the stream. Other players stopped killing each other and started killing freezers alongside Wendy's; top streamers like KingRichard joined in; Twitch itself cut a highlight reel of Wendy's best "freezer kills." Tens of thousands of gamers replicated the mission in their own gameplay and documented it as UGC. The stream amassed 1.5M+ minutes watched, 250K+ live views, and a 119% lift in Wendy's social mentions — and Epic's developers ultimately removed the burger freezers from the game entirely.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): Wendy's didn't buy its way into Fortnite — it found a story already inside the game's lore that only Wendy's could tell, so the intrusion read as fandom, not advertising. P03 (Behavior Inversion): in a last-person-standing shooter, refusing to kill players and attacking kitchen appliances instead was so absurdly against the grain that it became inherently watchable and repeatable by others. P14 (Product as Proof): every destroyed freezer was a dramatization of "fresh, never frozen" — the brand message was the gameplay, not a message layered on top of it. The nine-hour commitment mattered: a one-tweet gag would have been sideline commentary; nine hours of play earned player status. The ending — developers patching the freezers out — turned a stunt into permanent canon.

## Steal
Before paying to place your brand inside a culture, search that culture's existing fiction for a conflict your product truth can join. Then express your USP as an in-world action anyone can copy, not a claim — if fans can perform your message themselves, the community becomes your media plan.
