# Make Love Last (Viatris · 2024)

## Insight
In China, pharmaceutical advertising is strictly regulated and open discussion of sex remains culturally sensitive — a double lock on any ED-adjacent brand communication. The human truth underneath: intimacy naturally fades over time. As couples age, everyday pressure erodes closeness until they can't remember when they last truly connected. The deeper reframe: intimacy is not about physical performance but about sustaining a bond — which turns a taboo pharma subject into a universal love story.

## Mechanic
Viatris and Ogilvy Shanghai framed intimacy as art. Three real couples shared candid, unscripted stories about the challenges of staying connected. A renowned artist then captured hours of their genuine physical closeness using time-lapse photography, transforming fleeting moments of intimacy into enduring works of visual art. A week later, the couples were brought to a private gallery to encounter the artworks of their own closeness, reigniting the emotions on camera. The films and images were released online on Chinese Valentine's Day (Qixi), amplified by medical experts and relationship KOLs for credibility. Results: 350 million impressions, 85.8 million hero-film views, and 1.89 million organic discussions about love, intimacy, and reconnection — a public conversation on a culturally silenced topic.

## Why it worked
P06 (Limitation as Power): regulation forbade talking about the product, so the campaign talked about time instead — the constraint forced the work up a level, from performance to enduring connection, which is a stronger emotional territory anyway. P08 (Craft as Message): time-lapse is not decoration; long exposure IS the metaphor — love as something that accumulates and leaves a trace, "make love last" rendered literally in the medium. Real couples rather than actors kept a delicate subject authentic instead of embarrassing, and gallery framing gave audiences a tasteful, shareable way to discuss something they had no vocabulary for.

## Steal
When regulation or taboo forbids naming your product's function, find the poetic dimension of the same truth (duration instead of performance, care instead of cure) and let a craft technique embody it literally. Real subjects re-encountering their own recorded emotion is a reliable engine for authentic reaction footage.
