# Lynx with Catnip (Lynx · 2025)

## Insight
A date isn't won or lost on the human alone — a Censuswide study found 60% of cat owners would dump a partner their cat doesn't like. The cat is an unadvertised gatekeeper in modern dating, and no fragrance brand had ever tried to win over the pet as well as the person.

## Mechanic
Lynx launched the world's first body fragrance formulated for both humans and their cats: infused with nepetalactone, the compound in catnip that mimics feline pheromones. To prove the claim rather than just state it, Lynx installed a billboard in London's Crystal Palace Park — birthplace of the UK's National Cat Club and home to established stray cat colonies — rigged to release the fragrance and literally attract real, unscripted cats on camera. A launch film by director Andreas Nilsson carried the story into print, OOH, audio, guerrilla activations and social.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): rather than claiming cats would love the scent, Lynx staged a public, unscripted demonstration in a location cats actually already congregate — real cats showing up on camera is a claim no script could fake. P17 (Design as Idea): the product itself (a genuinely new fragrance formulation, not a repackaged existing scent) is the creative idea — the billboard stunt and film exist to prove a real R&D decision, not decorate an empty one.

## Steal
When your product claim can be staged as a live, unscripted demonstration in a real-world location where the proof already tends to show up on its own, build the campaign around capturing that moment rather than dramatizing a script — the audience trusts what visibly wasn't staged more than what was told to them.
