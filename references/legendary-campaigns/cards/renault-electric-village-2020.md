# Electric Village (Renault · 2020)

## Insight
The automotive industry spent close to a billion euros on ads trying to sell electric cars, yet only 7% of people were ready to buy one. The remaining 93% agreed the future is electric but believed they personally needed more range, more infrastructure, or a big-city life to make the switch. More advertising could not fix a belief about feasibility — only proof could. And the strongest proof isn't the easiest case; it's the hardest one: if the remotest village in France can go fully electric, then it's possible everywhere.

## Mechanic
Renault turned Appy — the remotest town in France, where driving is a daily necessity and electric seemed impossible — into the first 100% electric-vehicle city on Earth. Every single inhabitant's car was replaced with an all-electric Renault ZOE for three years. Journalists were invited to the inauguration and followed the inhabitants' lives; a 15-minute documentary was broadcast on TV as a one-week series and online. The reveal after months of driving: nothing changed. Patrick still commutes, Sylvie still does her groceries, the kids still get to school, Jean-Pierre still gets lost even with GPS. The plan extended to charging clean — fitting the village with photovoltaic installations on the mayor's office so the ZOEs charge on locally produced green energy, not grey energy.

## Why it worked
P12 (Social Experiment): a three-year, real-life experiment with a whole town as the sample — not a staged demo but a longitudinal proof that generated its own narrative. P14 (Product as Proof): the argument for the ZOE is the ZOE itself, performing under worst-case conditions; "nothing changed" is the most persuasive claim an anxious buyer can hear. The choice of Appy is the strategy: extreme-case logic converts one village into a universal syllogism ("if here, then everywhere"). Journalists began covering it a month before launch (€400K earned media pre-launch, €1M after; 63M views, 29M impressions), and within a year ZOE sales rose 50%, making Renault the best-selling EV brand in Europe — while Appy saved 7,000 litres of petrol and 10 tonnes of CO2.

## Steal
When the barrier is "that wouldn't work for someone like me," don't demo the average case — prove the extreme case and let the audience's own logic generalize it. Duration is credibility: a three-year commitment produces a claim ("nothing changed") that no 30-second spot can make. And cast real, lovable, ordinary people; warmth toward the protagonists becomes warmth toward the proposition.
