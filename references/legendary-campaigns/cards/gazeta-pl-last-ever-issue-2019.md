# The Last Ever Issue (Gazeta.pl · 2019)

## Insight
Poland scores 10 points below the EU average on gender equality, formal sex education is nearly absent from schools, and an autocratic government had rolled back women's rights. For 27 years, "Your Weekend" — Poland's longest-running and most iconic adult magazine — had taught generations of Polish men to see women as sex objects. The strategic insight targeted middle-class Polish men who didn't reject equality but believed sexism "wasn't their problem or their doing": they had been raised on a biased portrayal of women without ever realising it. You couldn't lecture them out of that bias — but you could buy the thing that built it.

## Mechanic
When "Your Weekend" came up for sale in December 2018, Gazeta.pl — teamed with Mastercard and BNP Paribas, two brands with long-term commitments to women's empowerment — bought it immediately, and used its final issue to end it. Published on International Women's Day 2019, "The Last Ever Issue" kept the magazine's familiar sections but reimagined every one: leading Polish female photographers offered diverse portrayals of womanhood; essays and interviews covered sexual education, gender portrayal, equal rights and sexism; the cover featured a writer, an acclaimed actress and an MMA world champion. A teaser week built anticipation, nationwide offline/online distribution ran through the publisher's networks, and 100+ copies sent to journalists and influencers ignited a fierce national debate. The magazine's social channels were converted into a long-term education platform. Results: zero objectifying impressions since 08/03/2019, 4.5M organic reach, 25M media impressions (in a country of 28M adults), the best-selling issue in 10 years, Poland's best-selling e-magazine of March 2019 — and a Titanium Grand Prix at Cannes 2019.

## Why it worked
P04 (Brand as Activist): Gazeta.pl didn't publish an opinion about sexism — it spent real money to acquire and kill a cultural institution of sexism, the strongest possible proof of conviction for a media brand whose role is shaping national conversation. P03 (Behavior Inversion): the perfect trojan horse — the audience most needing the message (men raised on the magazine) received it inside the very format that had shaped their bias, with the familiar sections intact so the substitution itself delivered the argument. The non-judgmental strategy mattered: it didn't accuse readers, it showed them what they'd been fed. And ending the title rather than reforming it made the act symbolic and absolute: "the last ever issue" is a headline, a product and a verdict in four words.

## Steal
When a cultural artifact perpetuates the problem your brand opposes, consider buying the artifact instead of buying media against it. Ownership converts commentary into action — and delivering the new message inside the old, trusted format reaches exactly the audience that would never click on a campaign about the issue.
