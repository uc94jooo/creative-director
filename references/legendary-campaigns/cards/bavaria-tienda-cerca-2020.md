# Tienda Cerca (Bavaria (AB InBev) · 2020)

## Insight
In Colombia, the traditional channel accounts for about 80% of Bavaria's volume, and neighborhood corner stores alone make up half of that — they are also the main source of income for millions of families. When the COVID lockdowns hit in March 2020, more than 18% of these stores closed within the first month. The stores had been frozen in time while the world digitized: they had loyal customers and full shelves, but no digital storefront. Bavaria already held the missing asset — a database of every corner shop registered as a client.

## Mechanic
Bavaria turned its client database into public infrastructure. Every registered corner store was mapped onto Google Maps with basic info, then connected through tiendacerca.com: customers shared their location, saw every shop around them, and placed orders with a single click via WhatsApp directly to their local grocer for home delivery. No app to install, no onboarding for shopkeepers — the platform ran on tools both sides already used. In the first week 60,000+ stores registered; in 60 days the site logged over 10 million visits; 70% of registered shops reported a sales increase, and allies contributed roughly 2 billion Colombian pesos to store infrastructure. The model was so simple that nine countries replicated it within six months, connecting over 400,000 stores.

## Why it worked
P07 (Invisible Brand): Bavaria built the largest hyper-local e-commerce in the country and put the shopkeepers, not the beer, at the center — the brand benefit (8-point beer volume uplift among participating stores) arrived as a by-product of saving its own retail channel. P04 (Brand as Activist): in a survival moment, the brand spent its data and reach on its most vulnerable partners rather than on messaging. The genius is the assembly: Google Maps + WhatsApp + an existing client database — zero new behaviour required from either side, which is why adoption exploded in weeks, not quarters.

## Steal
In a crisis, audit what your company already owns — databases, logistics, relationships — and ask what public-facing utility it becomes if you open it up. And build on the tools your users already have in their pocket: the fastest platform to scale is the one nobody has to learn.
