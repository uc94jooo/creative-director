# Project Revoice (The ALS Association · 2018)

## Insight
The Ice Bucket Challenge raised $115M+ for ALS research, but science takes time and patients needed their lives improved now. Within a couple of years of diagnosis most ALS patients are paralyzed and forced onto text-to-speech devices with a default computer voice — and as one patient put it, you don't realize how personal and unique your voice is until it's taken from you. Losing your voice isn't a communication problem; it's an identity amputation.

## Mechanic
With AI voice specialists (Lyrebird/Nakatomi), BWM Dentsu built Project Revoice: a deep learning algorithm that analyzes the "DNA" of a person's voice and creates a complete digital clone, integrated with the eye-tracking text-to-speech devices ALS patients already use — so they can keep speaking freely and naturally as themselves after they physically can't. Where existing tech required an hour a day of recording for a month, Revoice rebuilt the voice of Ice Bucket Challenge co-founder Pat Quinn from less than two hours of old ice-bucket interview footage. The launch film captured the moment Pat spoke in his own voice for the first time in over a year ("Time to say goodbye to this computerized voice"), and the program opened to any ALS patient at projectrevoice.org.

## Why it worked
P18 (Tech as Canvas): the AI isn't a gimmick layered onto a message — the technology is the entire idea, delivering the emotional payload (a man's voice returned) that no scripted film could fake. P14 (Product as Proof): instead of asking for more awareness, the ALS Association built a working service, proving the Ice Bucket money era translated into tangible quality-of-life change. Choosing Pat Quinn closed a perfect narrative loop — the man who gave ALS its loudest moment got his own voice back from that moment's archive footage. Results: 900M+ earned media reach, 1M+ organic views in week one, 41M+ people in the conversation by week two, 680+ articles globally.

## Steal
Audit what your audience has already given you — archives, recordings, data — and ask what technology can now return to them from it. And when launching a utility, don't demo the tool; film the single most emotionally loaded first use you can find, with the person whose story carries the brand's whole history.
