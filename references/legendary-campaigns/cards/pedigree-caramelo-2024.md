# Pedigree Caramelo (Pedigree · 2024)

## Insight
The Caramelo mutt is Brazil's favorite dog — star of memes, videos, songs, even a petition to put it on the currency. Yet Brazil's most popular "breed" isn't officially a breed at all: it's classified as a vira-lata (mutt), which makes it by far the most common dog in Brazilian shelters and roughly 90% less likely to be adopted than purebreds, in a country with some 30 million homeless animals. The brand unlock is a pun with teeth: if what Caramelos lack is a pedigree, who better to give it to them than Pedigree?

## Mechanic
The campaign ignited through engineered outrage: superstar Tatá Werneck (owner of 12 adopted pets) tried to enter her mutt in a dog show, was rejected for lacking a pedigree, and posted her indignation — which broke into culture before the brand ever appeared. Pedigree then stepped in and launched the initiative to give Caramelos everything purebreds have: the biggest canine genetic study ever done on a mutt (350 Caramelos, with DNA Pets) establishing shared physical and health traits; the Caramelo Kennel Club — the biggest kennel club in Latin America — issuing DNA-based pedigree certificates; the first-ever Caramelo Dog Show with a major adoption fair for newly certified dogs; and repackaged Pedigree products featuring, for the first time, a mixed-breed dog. A film ran across cinema, OOH, YouTube, Meta, and TikTok, backed by a squad of national celebrities. The FCI — the world's largest purebred dog organization — responded by announcing Caramelos would be allowed in its shows, and the most common mutt became the most adopted breed in Brazil.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): the Caramelo was already a beloved national symbol with meme-level cultural equity; Pedigree didn't build affection, it institutionalized affection that culture had already produced. P14 (Product as Proof): the brand name *is* the mechanic — Pedigree granting pedigrees collapses strategy, name, and cause into one self-evident move. P04 (Brand as Activist): rather than an awareness film about adoption, the brand built real infrastructure (genetic study, kennel club, certification, show) that dismantled the actual status barrier keeping mutts in shelters. The celebrity-outrage opening gave the story an authentic, earned entry point, and forcing a concession from the FCI turned the campaign into news twice.

## Steal
Look for a status system in your category (certifications, registries, awards, "official" anything) that excludes the majority your brand serves — then build the parallel institution that legitimizes them, ideally one only your brand name could credibly issue. And when your brand name doubles as the solution's noun, stop writing taglines: the pun that solves the problem is the campaign.
