# Magnetic Stories (Siemens Healthineers · 2024)

## Insight
An MRI is terrifying for a child: a loud, claustrophobic machine producing alien banging, beeping and pumping noises — and pediatric oncology patients may have to endure it every two months. But every MRI scan produces a unique, predictable sequence of sounds. If you can predict exactly when each noise will occur, you can write a story around it.

## Mechanic
Siemens Healthineers collected the sound sequences of the five most common pediatric MRI scans (cranial, liver, spine, sella turcica, ears) and partnered with renowned children's book authors and sound designers to produce audiobooks that sync story moments precisely with each machine sound. The fast-spin-echo beeping becomes a clumsy talking robot; the constant pumping of the helium recondenser becomes a flying train; a loud drone becomes a spaceship dodging lasers. Radiology technicians at CUF, Portugal's leading hospital network, integrated the recordings into MRI scanner set-ups so each audiobook plays in perfect sync with its scan. The stories were tested with children and market-researched with technicians and parents before rollout to all 25 CUF hospitals — and proved good enough to sell as standalone audiobooks on Kobo, outside the hospital entirely.

## Why it worked
P06 (Limitation as Power): the scariest feature of the product — its dreadful noise — is converted into the raw material of the solution, so the fear source becomes the delight source. P08 (Craft as Message): the idea only works because the sync is precise; the craftsmanship of matching narrative beats to acoustic signatures is the proof of Siemens' engineering intimacy with its own machines. For a B2B brand selling MRIs to hospitals, improving patient experience at the software-and-story layer demonstrates innovation leadership more vividly than another spec sheet — and for repeat pediatric patients, it compounds into genuine care-journey value.

## Steal
List the sensory byproducts of your product that everyone treats as unavoidable negatives — noise, wait time, smell, friction. Ask whether any of them is predictable enough to be scored, narrated, or choreographed. Turning a byproduct into content is cheaper than eliminating it and more memorable than apologizing for it.
