# Better on a Better Network (Telstra · 2024)

## Insight
Telstra, Australia's largest network, suffered the big-telco curse: perceived as a faceless corporation even though its genuine advantage — more coverage in more places — matters most to the small regional towns nobody advertises to. The Olympics offered a rare mass-attention window, but a single self-congratulatory network ad would confirm the "big and faceless" perception rather than fix it.

## Mechanic
Instead of one hero spot, Telstra made 26 stop-motion films, each a "come-to-life postcard" from a lesser-known regional town — Flinders Island, Tambo, Duck Hill, Oberon — proving life is better all the way out there on a better mobile network. Sets were meticulously researched and rebuilt in miniature; puppets with internal armatures were animated frame by frame with no post-production shortcuts; the voices were real locals from the towns depicted ("I hope they shoot me out of the cannon"). Each tiny micro-narrative built striking craft and then threw it all away on a silly little joke. The series ran across Channel 9's Olympics and Paralympics broadcasts, guaranteeing variety instead of repetition through four weeks of coverage.

## Why it worked
P08 (Craft as Message): the obsessive miniature stop-motion is the argument — a company that lovingly recreates Duck Hill in felt and wire cannot be faceless, and the effort spent on tiny towns literalises "coverage everywhere." P10 (Serialization & Ritual): 26 episodes turned a media buy into an anticipated series; viewers watched for which town came next, so high Olympic frequency became an asset instead of an irritant. Casting real underrepresented locals made regional Australians the heroes rather than the target. Result: a disproportionate share of voice — the equivalent of 4 years of sponsorship value compressed into 4 weeks — with lifted network-superiority perceptions especially among non-customers, plus fan homages and competitor parodies. Cannes Grand Prix winner.

## Steal
When you own a genuine functional advantage nobody feels emotionally, don't state it — dramatise its beneficiaries. Make many small love letters instead of one big boast, and let visible, disproportionate craft carry the sincerity while a self-deprecating joke keeps it lovable. Serialise inside a high-frequency media moment so repetition becomes collection.
