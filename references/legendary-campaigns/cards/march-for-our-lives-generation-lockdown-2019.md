# Generation Lockdown (March For Our Lives · 2019)

## Insight
American adults had developed school shooting burnout: mourn the news, say "never again," move on, repeat. The politicization of gun violence — every shooting instantly becoming a partisan media battle — drove people out of the conversation entirely and away from the humans affected. Meanwhile, more than 95% of students regularly practice lockdown drills, but almost no adult has ever experienced one. The generation gap wasn't in opinion; it was in lived experience.

## Mechanic
March For Our Lives brought a real lockdown drill to real staff at a real workplace — led by an 11-year-old instructor, Kayleigh, teaching adults the survival rules she learned at school: don't talk out loud, push tables against doors, don't cry because it gives away your hiding spot, stand on the toilet seat. The film of the session launched with $0 media spend, simultaneously on CNN and through social posts from MFOL student leaders under #GenerationLockdown. News channels, senators, members of Congress, and future Vice President Kamala Harris amplified it. Results: 22.8M video viewers in 7 days, 105M impressions in a month, a 396% increase in lockdown conversation, and 37,955 petition signatures.

## Why it worked
P03 (Behavior Inversion): the child teaches the adult — reversing the normal direction of protection makes the abnormality of the drills impossible to unsee. P12 (Social Experiment): staging a real drill with unsuspecting adults produces genuine, unactable reactions that carry the message better than any script. P13 (Truth Telling): nothing is invented — every instruction Kayleigh gives is verbatim from actual school training, which is what makes it devastating. The idea sidestepped the politicized debate entirely by transferring an experience instead of arguing a position.

## Steal
When an audience is numb to an issue, don't add another argument — transfer the lived experience of the people inside it to the people outside it. The most persuasive fact is often a routine the audience never knew existed, delivered by the person for whom it is normal.
