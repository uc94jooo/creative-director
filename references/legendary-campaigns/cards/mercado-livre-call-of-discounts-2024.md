# Call of Discounts (Mercado Livre · 2024)

## Insight
Gaming audiences are famously resistant to advertising — banner ads in games and sponsored streams are tolerated at best. But gamers will chase anything the game itself makes chaseable. Call of Duty's Prop Hunt mode — where players hide disguised as everyday objects — is, structurally, already a product showcase: crates, lamps, chairs. Mercado Livre's insight was that "everyday objects" is literally its catalogue, so the game mode could become a live shopping mechanic without changing a single rule.

## Mechanic
Mercado Livre and GUT São Paulo staged a live, playable shopping experience inside Call of Duty. In a custom Prop Hunt, Neymar Jr. disguised himself as household items sold on Mercado — a toaster, a lamp, an armchair. During live Twitch streams, gamers hunted for him; the faster they found Neymar, the bigger the discount unlocked on that exact product in real life. Play and purchase fused into one loop: spot the prop, win the code, buy the item.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): the campaign didn't sponsor gaming culture, it played by its rules — a beloved game mode, a live Twitch format, and Brazil's biggest icon inside the match, making the brand a participant rather than an interrupter. P01 (Format as Idea): Prop Hunt itself is the idea; the mechanic of hiding-as-objects maps one-to-one onto an e-commerce catalogue, so the media format and the sales channel are the same thing. The numbers prove the loop closed: 180,000+ concurrent viewers (Twitch's most-watched live event of the year), 16,000+ discount codes redeemed within 45 minutes, and over $1.3M in sales, with a sharp lift in brand awareness among young audiences.

## Steal
Look for game mechanics that already resemble your business model, then plug real inventory into them. The test: can the audience's in-game action directly trigger a real-world transaction? If the bridge needs explaining, it's sponsorship; if it needs no explaining, it's play.
