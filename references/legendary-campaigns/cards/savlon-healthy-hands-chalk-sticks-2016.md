# Savlon Healthy Hands Chalk Sticks (Savlon · 2016)

## Insight
Kids in rural India explore the world with their hands — and eat with them. Not washing hands with soap is a leading cause of illness and school dropouts. But teaching a new habit to millions of children through persuasion is slow and fragile. The unlock was hiding in the classroom itself: most primary-grade students in rural India still write with chalk sticks on black slates. Their hands are already covered in chalk powder every day before lunch. What if the chalk itself could close the loop?

## Mechanic
Savlon created Healthy Hands Chalk Sticks — chalk made from a mixture of chalk powder and soap granules. Kids write with them exactly like regular chalk; at lunch break, when they put their chalk-dusted hands under the tap, the powder turns into soap on its own. No lesson, no lecture, no new behavior required — the existing habit does the washing. The sticks rolled out to schools across the country with national TV news coverage, features in over 70 newspapers, and a reach of 4.3 million on Twitter and 3.2 million on Facebook in four days. Food-grade safety certification earned the biggest prize: Akshaya Patra, the world's largest provider of school midday meals, approached Savlon to distribute the chalks to more than a million children every day, free, alongside schools and NGOs.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): a hygiene brand's purpose ("healthy hands") is executed as a physical invention, not an awareness film — the product is simultaneously the message, the medium, and the intervention. P03 (Behavior Inversion): instead of fighting to create a new habit (wash with soap), it piggybacks on an existing, unavoidable one (writing with chalk), reducing the behavior-change cost to zero. That's the deepest version of nudge design: the child doesn't decide to wash with soap — it happens to them. Institutional adoption (Akshaya Patra, schools, NGOs) turned a campaign into permanent infrastructure.

## Steal
Before designing a persuasion campaign for a new behavior, map the behaviors your audience already performs daily and ask which one can be hijacked to deliver the outcome automatically. The best behavior change asks for no behavior change.
