# Saylists (Warner Music Group · 2021)

## Insight
One in twelve young people has some form of speech sound disorder, and the only reliable way to overcome one is repetition — practicing problem sounds over and over. The problem: for kids, repetition is boring, so they disengage from therapy. Except in one context where humans happily repeat sounds hundreds of times without noticing: singing along to pop music. The audience for pop music and the age group most likely to have a speech sound disorder are the same people.

## Mechanic
Warner Music teamed up with Apple Music and built an algorithm that analyses song lyrics for specific sounds occurring in patterns beneficial for speech therapy — frequency, proximity to similar sounds, placement within a sentence. It then ran the largest data analysis of lyrics ever, scanning over 70 million songs, scoring and ranking each for genuine therapeutic value. Top-scoring songs that fit the listener profile were compiled into "Saylists" — playlists categorised by problem sound (S, T, CH, D, etc.) — launched on Apple Music, free and available indefinitely, so kids practise the sounds they struggle with simply by singing along to songs they actually like. Within weeks, therapists were using them in sessions, the Royal College of Speech and Language Therapists endorsed the project, Cambridge University's English Language Teaching department published lesson plans based on them, El Mundo called it "Apple's next great invention," and expansion into other languages was greenlit.

## Why it worked
P01 (Format as Idea): the idea IS the format — a playlist, the most ordinary unit of music consumption, reframed as a medical device. Zero new behaviour is required from the child; the therapy hides inside something they already love, which solves the actual brief (engagement) rather than the surface brief (awareness). P14 (Product as Proof): Warner's CSR claim ("music has positive social impact") is proven by the catalogue itself — an existing dataset re-read through a therapeutic lens, echoing how linguistic analysis of therapy overlaps with analysis of lyrics. The utility is permanent and refreshable, making the brand's purpose an always-on service rather than a campaign.

## Steal
Take a dataset your brand already owns and re-analyse it with the toolkit of a completely different discipline (therapy, medicine, education) — unrealised utility is often sitting in existing assets. Then deliver the benefit inside a behaviour your audience already enjoys, so compliance costs them nothing.
