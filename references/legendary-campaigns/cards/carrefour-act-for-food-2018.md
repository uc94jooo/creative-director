# Act For Food (Carrefour · 2018)

## Insight
Carrefour invented the hypermarket and suffered from what the agency called the "big bad wolf syndrome": as the historic leader, consumers blamed it for everything wrong with industrial food — standardization, price wars, low-quality products. "When you're big, you're bad." That insidious equation didn't just hurt image; it prevented the company from believing it could transform. The reframe: leadership is not the problem, it's the lever. Put the leader's scale at the service of the food transition instead of the price war.

## Mechanic
Act For Food is a creative platform where every campaign is a concrete act, not a statement. Worldwide acts: changing a European law to re-authorize farmers' seeds (the Black Supermarket), co-creating the largest vegetarian label with customers, launching Loop reusable packaging for e-commerce, financing 60,000 farmers in an agroecology program, rebuilding the Carrefour own-label to higher standards, deploying blockchain traceability. Each country added local acts — converting 2,000 French farmers to organic, making slaughterhouse video surveillance legally mandatory, carving anti-inflation prices in steel on Brazilian shelves. Internally, Act For Change let any employee propose new acts. Progress is published alongside financial results in the annual report, and shareholders voted the mission into the company bylaws.

## Why it worked
P04 (Brand as Activist): the activism was structural, not rhetorical — two laws actually changed. P14 (Product as Proof): every claim was backed by a shipped act (a label, a program, a law), so the advertising never had to argue. P13 (Truth Telling): opening with "we have been part of the problem" bought the credibility every subsequent act spent. Results: fruit and vegetable sales +5%, organic +28%, vegetarian +100%, brand preference +8pts, stock value +9%, organic leadership in France. Four years on, the platform still runs — proof it was a business operating system, not a campaign. Cannes Grand Prix (Titanium) 2020/2021.

## Steal
When a leader brand is blamed for the category's sins, don't deny the size — repurpose it. Convert the marketing budget from claims into acts, make each act concrete enough to be a headline on its own, and admit past fault first. A platform of provable acts compounds; a purpose statement depreciates.
