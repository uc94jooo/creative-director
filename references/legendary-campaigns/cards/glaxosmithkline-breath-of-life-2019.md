# Breath of Life (GlaxoSmithKline · 2019)

## Insight
COPD is the third-largest cause of death in China — roughly 100 million sufferers, driven by smoking, second-hand smoke and industrial pollution — yet fewer than 7% are properly diagnosed, because shortness of breath is commonly mistaken for a natural part of aging and lung function tests are inaccessible. The behavioural key wasn't more warnings; it was radical simplicity: a screening tool so effortless and culturally native that it fits inside the platform people already use every day.

## Mechanic
Breath of Life turned the mobile phone into the first-ever COPD self-testing tool, housed as a mini program on WeChat (87% smartphone penetration in China). The phone's microphone records the sound of a forced exhale and converts it into a soundwave; an algorithm developed with a leading pulmonologist compares the height of the wave in one second against total height — the clinical ratio used in lung function testing. The result is rendered not as a score but as a tree in traditional Chinese blow-ink painting style, created with a well-known blow-painting artist: the user picks a tree style and flower colour, exhales, and the tree grows to the size of their approximate lung volume. A result below the healthy threshold triggers a hospital check-up recommendation. The bespoke trees were designed to be shared on social feeds, turning every test result into a prompt for others to test. Launched April 13, 2019; won a Grand Prix at Cannes 2019.

## Why it worked
P18 (Tech as Canvas): the phone microphone — hardware everyone already owns — was repurposed into medical screening infrastructure, collapsing the barrier between "I should get tested someday" and "I can test in ten seconds." P08 (Craft as Message): the blow-ink tree is the masterstroke — blow painting is itself an art form powered by breath, so the visualisation isn't decoration, it's a culturally resonant metaphor where the medium literally demonstrates the diagnosis. Reframing a frightening test result as a beautiful, shareable artwork removed the stigma and fear that suppress screening, and the share mechanic converted each user into media for the next.

## Steal
To make people do an avoided health behaviour, compress it into the platform they already open daily, and translate the clinical output into a culturally beautiful artifact people want to share. Ask: what sensor already in everyone's pocket could approximate our diagnostic — and what local art form makes the result feel like a gift instead of a verdict?
