# The First Speech (Reporters Without Borders · 2024)

## Insight
In the biggest election year in history — over half the world's population heading to the polls — democracy cannot be taken for granted, because tyranny never announces itself as tyranny. Every authoritarian in history was first elected or welcomed on the strength of a beautiful speech: promises of a free, prosperous, strong, civilized country, of working "openly and honestly," of protecting democracy. The words that precede repression are indistinguishable from the words of genuine hope. The only reliable way to tell them apart is not to listen to the words at all — it is to have a free press that holds power accountable. "Trust the free press. Not pretty words."

## Mechanic
For Reporters Without Borders Germany's 30th anniversary, INNOCEAN Berlin produced a series of cinematic films restaging the real first speeches of leaders who later turned authoritarian — hopeful crowds, stirring promises of democracy, prosperity and honesty, rendered in raw, poetic cinematography (DoP Konrad Losch) that lets the audience empathize with the believers in the crowd before the hindsight lands. The films launched in German cinemas and online, then spread across other RSF offices (Sweden streamed them in theatres and online). In more repressive environments the campaign proved its own point: Russia blocked web access to RSF's website within days of launch. The films drove donations for RSF Germany's anniversary year.

## Why it worked
P13 (Truth Telling): the campaign's evidence is documentary, not invented — these speeches were actually given, and the audience's own historical knowledge supplies the horror; RSF never has to say "he became a dictator." P05 (Cultural Hijack): it hijacks the most sacred genre of political communication — the inaugural speech — and turns it into a warning label. The structure creates a participatory chill: for the first minute you are seduced exactly as the original audience was, which makes the reveal an experience of your own credulity rather than a lecture about someone else's. And the Russian state blocking the campaign became the campaign's strongest proof point — censorship as earned media.

## Steal
When your message is "don't trust X," don't argue it — let the audience fall for X in real time and then reveal the hindsight. Archival truth beats invented drama: find the real historical footage or words that reframe themselves once context is added. And if an adversary suppresses your work, publicize the suppression; the attack is the testimonial.
