# Degree Inclusive (Degree · 2021)

## Insight
22 million Americans live with permanent disabilities affecting their arms or vision, and people with disabilities often apply deodorant multiple times a day — yet unscrewing a cap, twisting a stick, or pressing a spray can be difficult or impossible. Everyone else takes deodorant for granted; for this audience, a basic hygiene ritual requires help. Degree's stated purpose — "to inspire confidence in everyone to move more" — was contradicted by its own packaging. As one trial participant put it: "I never even thought about how hard it could be just to put deodorant on."

## Mechanic
Degree co-created the world's first inclusive deodorant with the disability community: a hooked, grippable design that is easier to handle, identify (with tactile/braille-style labeling), open, close, and apply — usable even without hands, via mouth or feet. It is also gender-neutral and refillable. 200 people with disabilities trialed the prototype and shaped concept, features, and messaging; NGO partners (Muscular Dystrophy Association, The Lighthouse Chicago, Open Style Lab) built credibility. The launch put disabled people front and center: the agency's own disabled designer was key media spokesperson, and two hero films told the stories of Nick, a barber and boxer with no hands, and Maria, who is blind and roller skates. Results: 2 billion earned impressions in 12 days, 130+ articles that were 100% positive, and organic engagement double the category average.

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): the brand's inclusive purpose is not claimed in a film — it is molded into the packaging; the product itself is the argument. P17 (Design as Idea): the redesign is the entire campaign; every story flows from the object. P06 (Limitation as Power): designing for the hardest use case produced a better product for everyone (including anyone losing mobility or vision with age), reframing accessibility as design leadership rather than charity. The communication was inclusive in process, not just in message — co-created, disabled-led, and framed as empowerment rather than pity, which is why the disabled community amplified it in droves.

## Steal
Audit where your product's physical design contradicts your brand's stated purpose — that gap is a bigger idea than any campaign. Then co-create the fix with the excluded audience and let them lead the storytelling; "designed for us, by us" earns media that "designed for you" never will.
