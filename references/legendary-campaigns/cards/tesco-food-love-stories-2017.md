# Food Love Stories (Tesco · 2017)

## Insight
The UK's leading supermarket (3,500 stores) was losing ground to budget rivals in tough economic circumstances — and a price war against discounters is a war a premium-scale grocer cannot win. The escape route was a human truth the discounters weren't using: people love to cook food for the people they love. Food isn't a basket of priced items; it's the medium of relationships.

## Mechanic
BBH London built a serialized storytelling platform: each execution told the story of a named, real-feeling person and the dish they make for someone they love, putting a personal face on Tesco products. Launched in early 2017 for a two-year run across TV, print, outdoor and radio, the campaign reached 99% of the UK through five key media ingredients: brand-building stories in leading channels; data-personalized story selection to add meaning; localized up-weighting of outdoor and radio in regions where quality perception needed reinforcing; in-store recipe cards and the Tesco magazine so every featured meal could actually be cooked; and a profitability loop where the best-performing stories were singled out and rerun.

## Why it worked
P10 (Serialization & Ritual): "Food Love Stories" is a format, not an ad — an endlessly refillable container where every new dish and relationship is a fresh episode under one brand idea, which is why it sustained years rather than months. P16 (Long-form Drama): shifting from price claims to character-led stories moved the battlefield from wallet to heart, where discounters couldn't follow. The media system made emotion accountable: personalization by data, regional up-weighting, and rerunning proven stories turned sentiment into £679M of new revenue in 8 months, a 49% increase in media-driven sales, and a one-fifth improvement in quality scores (three-quarters attributable to the campaign). Cannes Grand Prix winner; it coincided with Tesco's first full year of growth in seven years.

## Steal
When challengers attack on price, change the question: find the emotional job your product actually performs (food = expressed love) and build a repeatable story format around it. Then close the loop to commerce — recipe cards, ingredients in-store — so the story is shoppable, and treat your stories as a portfolio: measure, rerun winners, and let data decide which emotions get more media.
