# DO Black (Doconomy · 2019)

## Insight
The 2018 UN report demanded carbon emissions be cut in half by 2030. The average Swedish consumer causes ~10 tons of CO2 a year — 60% of it linked to consumption — and while the emotional driver around climate is real and data-substantiated, consumers lack any concrete, tangible tool to act on it. Meanwhile the "premium credit card" is the financial industry's ultimate symbol of encouraged overconsumption: for decades, cards have limited only our credit, never our impact.

## Mechanic
Swedish fintech Doconomy launched DO Black, the world's first credit card with a CO2-emission limit: it stops you from overspending based not on available funds but on the carbon impact of what you buy. Three integrations make it work: the Åland Index assigns a verified CO2 value to every transaction; a per-country, per-capita emission ceiling is calculated from UN and Our World in Data public data, aligned with the UN 2030 target of a 50% reduction; and the limit overrides the account's financial credit level inside Mastercard's payment infrastructure. Launched with zero paid media — earned and partner channels only — it drew 20M+ earned reach, thousands of registrations, UNFCCC endorsement, 40+ banks requesting white-label collaboration, and Mastercard calling Doconomy "the future of sustainable payments." Grand Prix at Cannes 2019.

## Why it worked
P03 (Behavior Inversion): a premium card that stops you spending inverts the category's core promise — the black card, symbol of limitless consumption, becomes the symbol of limits. P14 (Product as Proof): the position "consumption has a cost" isn't a message, it's enforced at the point of payment; the product is the entire argument. P17 (Design as Idea): the design decision — swap the credit limit for a carbon limit — is one sentence long, instantly understood, and industry-shaking, which is why it traveled on earned media alone. It also converts abstract tonnage into the most familiar behavioral interface on earth: a declined card.

## Steal
Find the standard limit, meter or threshold in your category (credit limits, data caps, speed limits, calorie counts) and swap the unit it measures for the unit your purpose cares about. Enforcement beats encouragement: a tool that physically stops the behavior is a stronger story than any pledge — and if the mechanism is one sentence, earned media becomes your media plan.
