# ThisAbles (IKEA · 2019)

## Insight
IKEA's stated purpose is "creating a better everyday life for as many people as possible" — yet for 1 in 10 people living with serious disabilities, IKEA simply wasn't an option. They faced a humiliating trade-off: functional furniture that screams "medical equipment," or beautiful furniture they cannot physically use. As one man with cerebral palsy put it, his own home was full of furniture crying out that he couldn't sit on a regular sofa without fearing he wouldn't be able to get up. The audience itself surfaced the fix: it's the small things manufacturers miss that lock people out.

## Mechanic
IKEA hacked its own furniture. Through a one-week hackathon where the target audience became advisors — alongside product engineers, accessibility experts, psychologists and IKEA designers — the team created 13 small add-ons that make essential IKEA items accessible: sofa elevators for easier ascent, a super-zipper for pillow covers, lamp button enlargements for easy lighting. Each add-on is freely downloadable and 3D-printable anywhere in the world (public libraries increasingly have printers), matched IKEA's clean design language so nothing "sets anyone apart," and shipped with a manual so the designs could be recreated globally. Results: one of the biggest organic international coverage waves in Israeli brand history — $4M PR value, 489M unique-user reach, features in Fast Company, The Verge and The Independent; 45,000+ visitors from 127 countries to ThisAbles.com, thousands of downloads and prints.

## Why it worked
P17 (Design as Idea): the campaign *is* thirteen pieces of industrial design — the message "IKEA is for everyone" isn't claimed, it's shipped as printable objects. Keeping the add-ons visually consistent with IKEA's aesthetic was the emotional masterstroke: accessibility without stigma, "nothing as cool as this" versus decades of medicalized solutions. P14 (Product as Proof): by modifying its own catalogue rather than launching a charity initiative, IKEA proved the purpose inside the core product experience. Open-sourcing via 3D printing made an 8-million-person market's idea instantly global at near-zero distribution cost.

## Steal
Audit who is silently excluded from your product, then fix it with the smallest possible additions co-designed with the excluded audience — and release the fix as an open, replicable format so the world can scale it for you. Dignity means the accessible version must be as beautiful as the original.
