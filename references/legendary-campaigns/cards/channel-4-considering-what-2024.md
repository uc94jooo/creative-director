# Considering What? (Channel 4 · 2024)

## Insight
The Paralympics is the world's third biggest sporting event, yet 60% of viewers watched primarily to see athletes "overcoming disability" — inspiration tourism, not sport. Paralympians and the disabled community found this patronising. The reframe: the forces that define elite sport — gravity, friction, time — are totally indifferent to disability. They break bones and hearts no matter who you are. So when a viewer says "she's doing so well, considering" — considering what? If sport's forces don't care about disability, why do you?

## Mechanic
For its Paris 2024 Paralympics coverage, Channel 4's in-house agency 4creative (directed by Steve Rogers, Biscuit Filmworks) built the film around the forces themselves, personified as ruthless antagonists: Gravity "wants to introduce your face to the floor," Friction "is keeping your skin as a souvenir," Time "says you're not going to qualify." Immersive visual worlds — upside-down sets, onboard bike cameras, unrelenting edits — showed Paralympians battered by exactly the same brutal physics as any elite athlete. Then the film turned on the audience directly, replaying the condescending phrases ("he's so brave," "good for her," "amazing for someone in a wheelchair") against footage that made them absurd.

## Why it worked
P13 (Truth Telling): it named the audience's own patronising behavior out loud instead of politely working around it — the discomfort was the message. P02 (Enemy or Conflict): by casting gravity, friction, and time as the true opponents, disability was structurally deleted from the narrative; the athletes fight what every athlete fights. This continued Channel 4's decade-long Paralympic voice (Meet the Superhumans, We're the Superhumans) but escalated from celebrating athletes to confronting viewers. The opening ceremony drew 6 million viewers — the most-watched since London 2012 — coverage reached 19.1 million, and 79% of viewers came to see the Paralympics as equally thrilling as the Olympics.

## Steal
When your audience holds a well-meaning but condescending attitude, don't correct it with positivity — quote it back verbatim next to evidence that shames it. And when a group is defined by a difference, find the antagonist that ignores that difference entirely; a common enemy reframes "them" as "us" faster than any inclusion message.
