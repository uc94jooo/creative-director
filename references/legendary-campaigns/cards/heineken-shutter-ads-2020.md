# Shutter Ads (Heineken · 2020)

## Insight
The pandemic hit Heineken hard — lockdowns wiped out almost 70% of revenue — but it hit bars far worse: up to 60% faced permanent closure, and people were forgetting their local hangouts. Bars are Heineken's essential trade partner; if they die, the brand's home field dies with them. The closed steel shutter — the very symbol of a bar going dark — was also thousands of square meters of unused canvas in prime street locations. What if the sign of closure could fund the reopening?

## Mechanic
Heineken redirected its existing outdoor media budget from billboards to the closed shutters of bars, paying each bar media fees for the space exactly when they had zero revenue. Over 5,000 of the most loved bars across Argentina (Buenos Aires, La Plata, Rosario, Córdoba, Mar del Plata), Spain (Barcelona, Madrid, Valencia, Bilbao, Cádiz), Italy (Milan) and Germany (Berlin, Hamburg, Cologne, Munich) each received a bespoke ad painted for their shutters, displayed 24/7. Sister brands Amstel and Cruzcampo joined — and even competitors Guinness and Paulaner adopted the idea. €7.5 million went directly to bars; the shutters delivered 40% more media value than traditional OOH, and 100% of participating bars reopened.

## Why it worked
P06 (Limitation as Power): the campaign's medium is the crisis itself — the closed shutter, the most visible symptom of the problem, becomes the mechanism of the solution. No new budget was needed; only the redirection of a line item. P04 (Brand as Activist): instead of a message of solidarity, Heineken performed solidarity through its media plan — the purpose claim is auditable in euros. The idea also passes the ultimate flattery test: competitors copying it made it bigger, not weaker, because Heineken owned the authorship of a category-wide gesture.

## Steal
In a crisis, audit your existing budget lines before asking for new ones: the most credible purpose campaign is a reallocation, not a donation. And look for media space that is itself the symbol of the problem — advertising there makes the medium carry half the message.
