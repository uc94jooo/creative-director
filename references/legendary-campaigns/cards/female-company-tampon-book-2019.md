# The Tampon Book (The Female Company · 2019)

## Insight
In Germany, caviar, truffles and oil paintings were taxed at the reduced 7% rate while tampons carried the top 19% VAT — a "luxury goods" classification written by all-male lawmakers 50 years earlier. Other countries had abolished the tampon tax; German politicians refused to act. But the tax code held its own contradiction: books are also taxed at 7%. If tampons were sold inside a book, the sexist law could be beaten with the law itself.

## Mechanic
Scholz & Friends packaged 15 organic tampons inside "The Tampon Book" — 45 pages of bold illustrations and empowering stories about menstruation, taboo and feminism — legally sold at 7% VAT. The book promoted a change.org petition urging parliament to debate abolishing the tampon tax, making every purchase a political statement. With zero media budget, the launch (16 April 2019) ran on pure PR mechanics: 100 copies sent to politicians, 100 to feminist influencers and journalists, plus a trailer film on Instagram, Facebook and YouTube. More than half the contacted influencers shared it for free; Facebook temporarily banning the content only sharpened the story.

## Why it worked
P17 (Design as Idea): the packaging is the entire argument — the object itself demonstrates the absurdity that ink on paper is essential but menstrual products are luxury. P06 (Limitation as Power): the unjust tax law wasn't the obstacle, it was the raw material; "we outsmarted the law with the law itself" is the headline the media wrote for free. P04 (Brand as Activist): a small online shop took the lead of a national feminist movement, converting commerce into protest. Results: first edition sold out in a day, second (6,000 copies) in a week; 10.5M+ film views on zero spend; coverage from RTL and Pro7/Sat1 to national newspapers; within two weeks politicians invited the founders to join forces, and the petition passed 150,000 signatures — forcing parliament to officially debate the tax. Cannes PR Grand Prix 2019. (Germany cut the tampon tax to 7% in 2020.)

## Steal
Read the rules that oppress your audience until you find their internal contradiction, then build a product that exploits it — a legal hack embodied in a physical object is simultaneously proof, protest and press release. Price of entry: make buying the product the act of protest, so your revenue and your movement grow from the same transaction.
