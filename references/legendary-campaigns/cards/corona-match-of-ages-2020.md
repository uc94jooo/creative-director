# The Match of Ages (Corona · 2020)

## Insight
Corona México has always lived alongside soccer — and the pandemic took both away at once: leagues suspended, Mexicans locked down, and Corona losing 12.5 million gallons in sales during the week the classic derby should have been played. You can't film anything, produce anything, or gather anyone. But seventy years of match footage already exists. The game Mexico was missing could be edited into existence.

## Mechanic
We Believers created a brand-new 90-minute match between Mexico's two biggest rivals, cut entirely in post-production from 70 hours of historical footage spanning 1950–2020 — the best plays, goals, and superstars of all time sharing one pitch. Twenty editors worked 24/7 for 45 days, matching players' moves, field position, and speed so the eras stitched into one continuous game. In partnership with Televisa, it aired on primetime Canal de las Estrellas and streamed live on YouTube on June 7, 2020 — the same day the real classic should have been played. The historical billboards inside the footage were re-sold to real brands, with the money donated to fight Covid-19.

## Why it worked
P01 (Format as Idea): the invention was the format itself — a live broadcast of a match that never happened, treated with full match-day ritual (primetime slot, real commentary, real ad boards). P16 (Long-form Drama): ninety uninterrupted minutes of entertainment gave locked-down fans the actual thing they missed, not an ad about missing it. The emotion was precisely melancholic joy — the delight of watching legends of every era play together, sharpened by knowing none of it was real and why it had to be made. Results: #1 trending topic in Mexico, 54% increase in weekly sales, 79% more social mentions than any real classic, 98% positive sentiment, Corona México's highest earned media ever — and a reusable entertainment platform for other markets and sports. Cannes Grand Prix 2020/2021.

## Steal
When circumstances cancel the thing your audience loves, don't sympathize — reconstruct it from the archive. Brands sitting near decades of cultural footage can remix history into new live events. And honor the ritual details (broadcast slot, commentary, even the ad boards): fidelity to the ritual is what makes a synthetic event feel real.
