# Nigrum Corpus (IDOMED · 2025)

## Insight
Racism in Brazil's medical system is not a matter of isolated bad actors but of trained perception: Black patients endure microaggressions, indifference, and substandard care that the system itself has learned not to see. IDOMED (Instituto de Educação Médica), drawing on real-life experiences and the findings of the Mediversidade project (Instituto Yduqs/IDOMED), targeted the root: the education of the doctors themselves — because bias absorbed in medical school is bias practiced for a forty-year career.

## Mechanic
A dual-format campaign. The short film *Corpo Preto* follows a Black man through medical settings where he is met with microaggressions and cold, detached care — and cinematically, the patient is deliberately kept blurred in frame while the healthcare professionals stay sharp, making the systemic invisibility of Black patients literal on screen. It premiered April 1 at Cinema Estação, Gávea (Rio de Janeiro), followed by a panel with Dr. Amanda Machado and director Nany Oliveira, then amplified through OOH, influencer activations, lectures and public screenings. The second format is the book *Nigrum Corpus — Um estudo sobre racismo na medicina brasileira*, grounded in real testimonies and distributed to medical schools nationwide as a teaching tool — embedding racial literacy into the curriculum rather than leaving it at awareness.

## Why it worked
P13 (Truth Telling): the campaign refuses metaphor-as-softener — it names the problem in real testimonies and puts the audience (medical students and professionals) in front of documented experience they cannot dismiss as anecdote. P08 (Craft as Message): the blur is the idea. Making the protagonist literally out of focus turns a cinematography choice into the campaign's thesis — the audience feels the erasure before any line of dialogue explains it. The film creates the visceral confrontation; the book converts the emotion into institutional infrastructure. Awareness campaigns fade; a text adopted by medical schools compounds across every graduating class.

## Steal
When fighting a systemic bias, pair the visceral artifact (film that makes the invisible felt) with the institutional artifact (a tool the system must adopt — curriculum, protocol, textbook). And ask whether your craft decision can *enact* the insight rather than illustrate it: shoot the erasure, don't narrate it.
