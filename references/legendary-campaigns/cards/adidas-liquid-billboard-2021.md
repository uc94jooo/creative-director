# Liquid Billboard (Adidas · 2021)

## Insight
32% of women worldwide don't feel comfortable swimming in public; in the Middle East that number rises to 88%. Raised in societies where swimming isn't widely accepted as appropriate for women — and surrounded by ads showing only models and professional athletes — women in MENA experience water as judgment and restriction, not freedom. As one participant put it: "I never saw women that looked like me wearing the hijab, and I feel like that hindered my ability to dream." The very medium that had excluded them (outdoor advertising) was the place to reverse it.

## Mechanic
To launch its inclusive swimwear collection in Dubai, Adidas built the world's first swimmable billboard: a 5-meter-high, 3-meter-deep transparent pool holding 11,500 gallons of water, constructed on the beach in under three weeks. Any woman — regardless of shape, ethnicity, ability or faith — could dive in. Underwater cameras turned each swimmer's dive into a personalized Adidas poster, and every dive was live-streamed onto the city's largest digital display at Dubai Mall. Women who had only ever seen athletes and models on billboards saw themselves celebrated the same way, in real time, at landmark scale. Results: 350 million total reach, $6 million earned media, coverage in 60+ countries across 6 continents — and the Outdoor Grand Prix at Cannes Lions 2022.

## Why it worked
P01 (Format as Idea): the billboard doesn't advertise the message — it is the message; a swimmable ad literalizes "water is for you" in a way no film could. P09 (User as Co-Author): every participant became the campaign's talent, converting the audience with the deepest barrier into its most credible ambassadors, and the personalized posters gave each woman proof of her own belonging. The tension design is precise: the campaign asks women to do in public the exact thing 88% feel unable to do — but wraps the ask in celebration rather than challenge, with the brand's inclusive product as the enabling costume.

## Steal
Take the medium your audience associates with their exclusion and rebuild it so they can physically enter it. And when representation is the barrier, don't cast diverse talent — build a machine that makes the audience the talent, with a personalized artifact they keep.
