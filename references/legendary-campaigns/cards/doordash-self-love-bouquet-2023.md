# Self Love Bouquet (DoorDash · 2023)

## Insight
Every delivery service fights over the same Valentine's customer: traditional couples. But 1 in 4 women now spend Valentine's Day alone, 52% of DoorDash's customers are women, and 72% of US adults consider self-pleasure a form of "therapy" — yet female self-pleasure remains weirdly taboo, and single women get no cultural permission to enjoy the holiday. Rather than shout into the couples conversation alongside Uber Eats and 1-800-Flowers, DoorDash could start its own: take "self-love" literally.

## Mechanic
DoorDash launched the Self Love Bouquet, exclusively on the platform: 11 real red roses plus one Rose™ — the best-selling female sex toy of the previous five years — hidden in plain sight inside the most iconic Valentine's gift. The key message: be your own Valentine, buy yourself flowers. The stunt targeted 18–30 existing customers and converted a demographic that never buys flowers on the holiday — single people. Results: sold out in 4 days, 749M total impressions, 963K social impressions, 39K mentions, $17M gross merchandise volume, and $6M in local florists' order volume (double the prior year). PR Grand Prix, Cannes 2023.

## Why it worked
P05 (Cultural Hijack): it commandeers the most codified gift ritual on the calendar — the red-rose bouquet — and reroutes it from couples to singles at the exact moment the culture is primed. P11 (Absurd as Carrier): the wink of a sex toy disguised as a twelfth rose makes a taboo topic shareable and news-friendly without being crude — the joke carries the destigmatization. P03 (Behavior Inversion): flowers, the thing you receive from someone else, become the thing you send yourself. Crucially it wasn't just conversation: the product put self-love into practice and moved real commerce, including doubling local florists' volume.

## Steal
When your category converges on one audience at a seasonal peak, serve the audience the occasion structurally excludes — they're unserved, newsworthy and grateful. And to break a taboo at scale, smuggle it inside the occasion's most familiar icon: the cliché is the Trojan horse that gets the press release opened.
