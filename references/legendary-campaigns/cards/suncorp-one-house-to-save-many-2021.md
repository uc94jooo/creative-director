# One House To Save Many (Suncorp Group · 2021)

## Insight
Every Australian storm season, hundreds of thousands of homes are destroyed by cyclones, floods and bushfires — in 2020 alone, over 134,000 were damaged — and climate change is making entire North Queensland communities uninsurable. The systemic absurdity: 97% of disaster funding is spent on repair and rebuild, only 3% on prevention. Australians pick up the pieces, rebuild the same way, and hope it won't happen again — because building codes simply aren't adequate for the climate that now exists. For an insurer, the ultimate product isn't a payout; it's a house that doesn't need one.

## Mechanic
Suncorp partnered with the CSIRO, James Cook University and Room 11 Architects to design, scientifically test and build "One House" — the world's first home engineered to withstand cyclones, floods and bushfires while preserving the Australian way of life: mesh screens against fire and debris, water-diverting retaining walls, air-pressure relief systems against cyclone damage, every feature lab-tested against the worst Australia could throw at it. The project launched as a fully integrated campaign (brand and product TV, OOH, print, social, radio, digital utility) anchored by a prime-time documentary on Channel Nine; afterwards the designs were released open-source, teaching any Australian to build resilience into their own home, and presented to government and national building companies. Results: 99% target-audience reach, 20M+ earned impressions, every major primetime news station covering it — and business impact including consideration up 40%→45%, quotes +24.6%, market share +7.3%, and record NPS (6.4→14.4).

## Why it worked
P14 (Product as Proof): instead of claiming commitment to resilience, Suncorp built the physical artifact that proves it — a testable, filmable, walkable demonstration that repositioned the brand from insurance to resilience leadership. P02 (Enemy or Conflict): the enemy is precisely framed — not weather, but the 97/3 funding logic and outdated building codes — which gives media, government and industry a fixable villain rather than a fatalistic one. The idea also kept working after the campaign: Suncorp's "Build It Back Better" product applies One House learnings to every rebuilt home, the Insurance Council launched Project Resilience to embed resilience in the National Construction Code, and the Federal Government announced a $600m resilience fund.

## Steal
When your category profits from fixing damage, build the prototype that prevents it — the apparent self-cannibalization is exactly what makes the commitment credible and newsworthy. And design the artifact for three audiences at once: emotional proof for consumers, an evidence pack for journalists, and a policy blueprint for government.
