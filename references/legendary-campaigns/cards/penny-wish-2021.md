# The Wish (Penny · 2021)

## Insight
In Germany, teenagers were among those who suffered most from pandemic restrictions: homeschooling, contact bans and long lockdowns effectively cancelled teenage life. Their missed formative experiences — the parties, the first love, the rebellion — were never addressed in public, and the impact on a generation's mental health was underestimated. The sharpest way to voice it was through a parent, inverting the Christmas-wish convention: what a mother wants most for Christmas is for her son to get his youth back — including all the trouble a parent normally dreads.

## Mechanic
A four-minute Christmas film built as an intimate chamber play in the very scene of the pandemic: the family home. A teenage son asks his mother what she wants for Christmas, and her answer is a monologue of inverted wishes: "I wish you wouldn't hang out at home all the time… that you'd sneak out at night… that your dad would have to pick you up because you drank way too much… that girl would break your heart." A seamless tracking shot moves through the house as the wished-for scenes materialize — parties, falling in love, travelling — carried by a reinterpretation of Bon Jovi's "It's My Life", ending on the line: "I wish for you to get your youth back."

## Why it worked
P16 (Long-form Drama): four minutes of escalating emotional structure — an entire coming-of-age drama compressed into one monologue and one continuous camera move, with craft (direction by Marcus Ibanez, cinematography by Paul Özgür) doing the heavy lifting; it won the Film Craft Grand Prix at Cannes 2022. P13 (Truth Telling): it said out loud what German society had not — that a generation lost something irreplaceable — and said it from the least expected mouth. The inversion is the engine: every wish is something a "good mother" should never wish for, which is exactly why each one lands as an act of love. A discount supermarket bought itself a place in the national conversation by being the first brand to grieve on teenagers' behalf.

## Steal
When a collective loss goes unacknowledged, the brand that names it first owns the emotion. Find the socially forbidden sentence a loving person would never say ("I wish you'd drink too much") and let the context flip it into the deepest possible care. Inverting a ritual format (the Christmas wish list) gives you structure for free.
