# Plug-Inn (Renault · 2022)

## Insight
By 2022 there were 1 million electric cars on French roads but only 80,000 public charging stations, most concentrated in cities. Rural and remote drivers faced real range anxiety, which slowed the thermic-to-electric switch precisely where it needed public infrastructure the most. Building enough public stations to close the gap would take years and get tangled in municipal bureaucracy. But France already had a latent, invisible network that outnumbered public stations nine to one: 680,000 private home chargers sitting unused most of the day.

## Mechanic
Renault built Plug-Inn, a peer-to-peer app that works like Airbnb for EV charging: home charger owners list their charger and earn money renting it out, while drivers search, book, and pay to charge at a stranger's driveway wherever their route takes them — including remote areas with zero public stations. Renault fully financed the platform without waiting for public infrastructure investment or regulatory approval, turning a systemic industry bottleneck into a new peer-to-peer business model that could scale itself as more owners joined.

## Why it worked
P07 (Invisible Brand): Renault doesn't advertise the car, it builds the infrastructure the car needs — the brand's contribution is a utility, not a message. P18 (Tech as Canvas): a simple peer-to-peer app becomes the delivery mechanism for a national-scale problem, using existing smartphone and payment infrastructure rather than physical construction. P06 (Limitation as Power): the very absence of public charging stations in remote France becomes the design constraint that makes the private, distributed home-charger network more valuable than a centralized one would have been. The idea reframes a competitive weakness (charging infrastructure lag) into Renault's proof point as the EV category leader.

## Steal
When your industry's growth is capped by infrastructure nobody controls, look for the private, distributed version of that infrastructure that already exists at scale but sits idle. A peer-to-peer model can out-run public buildout timelines because it monetizes existing assets instead of waiting for new ones to be built.
